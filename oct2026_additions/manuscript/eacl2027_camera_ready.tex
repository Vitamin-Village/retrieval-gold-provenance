\begin{abstract}
Graph retrieval is reported to beat lexical retrieval on knowledge-grounded question answering, but in many benchmarks the retrieval gold derives from the knowledge graph (KG) the graph system retrieves over. We define an axis-specific provenance-circularity score fixed at construction and audit eleven benchmarks with a quote-backed rubric. Four non-author humans and eleven language-model raters place four of the six KG-constructed benchmarks in the two highest of five circularity bins. Controlled swaps that change only a retriever's KG measure the consequence. On 2WikiMultiHop, replacing the benchmark's own evidence structure with an independently extracted KG lowers hit@5 by $0.10$--$0.62$ for our retrievers and $0.20$--$0.28$ for HippoRAG~2 over three splits, and exact match falls with it. The retrieval drop replicates on MetaQA and GraphRAG-Bench. Across $30$ benchmark--split--retriever cells, graph retrieval significantly beats BM25 in $24$ with the evidence structure and in $5$ with the independent KG. The gold structure's entity-to-passage links and evidence edges carry the inflation, which persists when the reader knows neither the answer nor its evidence. On 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA, gold-source structures not induced from the evaluated questions inflate only a pure personalized-PageRank retriever, by $0.06$--$0.11$ hit@5.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Retrieval-augmented systems that use explicit structure built over a corpus, such as knowledge graphs (KGs) and entity indexes, are reported to outperform retrieval without such structure on knowledge-intensive tasks such as question answering (QA), query-focused summarization, and multi-hop retrieval~\citep{edge2024graphrag,gutierrez2024hipporag}. The standard interpretation is that structure helps a system reason across documents. We show that this interpretation is confounded when the structure a system ranks over links almost only to gold passages, as it does when the system ingests a benchmark's released evidence structure.

Consider a benchmark whose gold supporting facts are obtained by traversing a KG $\kg$, evaluated with a graph retriever that ranks passages using the same $\kg$. The retriever and the gold are then two views of one object. The graph method can score well because the target was defined in terms of the structure it uses, not because it finds evidence that lexical retrieval misses. We call this \emph{provenance circularity} (Figure~\ref{fig:circularity}).

Its consequence can be measured directly. We hold questions, gold, passages, and the lexical baseline fixed and replace the gold-aligned KG that a graph retriever ranks over by an independently extracted one. On 2WikiMultiHop this lowers a hybrid retriever's hit@5 by a paired $0.160$, that of two retrievers based on personalized PageRank (PPR) by $0.315$--$0.355$, and that of the published HippoRAG~2 system by $0.240$. Edge deletion separates our retrievers. For the hybrid retriever the gain survives the deletion of every edge of the aligned KG; for the PPR-based retrievers it vanishes, because the edges they walk are the gold evidence triples.

Prior work on benchmark reliability targets answer-side shortcuts in multi-hop QA~\citep{min2019compositional,trivedi2020dire,trivedi2022musique} and bias in language model (LM) judges for graph retrieval~\citep{judgebias2025}. Others have noted in passing that a retriever benefits from the KG a dataset was built on~\citep{sun2024thinkongraph} or from re-inserted ground-truth edges~\citep{cattaneo2026gtsubgraphs}. To our knowledge, none names retrieval-gold provenance as a construction-time property, scores it, or audits it per gold-unit axis across benchmarks.

We separate provenance circularity from three related notions. It is not a construction error: gold copied from a KG can be entirely correct and still circular. An error-propagation account predicts noisy gold, but not which retriever family gains, on which axis, or by how much; it also attributes to noise a gain that we trace to correct gold structure. Nor is circularity a matter of human versus automatic annotation: an LM-written question grounded in source text is provenance-independent, whereas a human-written semantic parse executed against a knowledge base (KB) yields KG-derived gold. Nor is it training contamination, which concerns whether a system saw the benchmark or its KG during training. We measure the readers' exposure separately and find no evidence that it carries the effect.

Our central claim is that retrieval-gold provenance is more than a descriptive property of how a benchmark was built. When a graph retriever reuses gold-derived structure, the alignment causally changes retrieval and answer scores. Scoring the same retriever against a provenance-independent structure separates that change from genuine retrieval gains. We ask three questions. How prevalent is provenance circularity across knowledge-grounded QA benchmarks, and on which gold-unit axes (RQ1)? Does alignment between the gold and the retrieval structure causally inflate retrieval scores and the answer accuracy measured downstream (RQ2)? What carries the inflation, and does running the same retriever over a provenance-independent KG remove it (RQ3)?

We make four contributions. (i)~An axis-specific, construction-time provenance-circularity score, a quote-backed rubric, and a census of eleven benchmarks. For every KG-constructed benchmark the derived-versus-independent reading is exact or traced to primary sources. All four non-author humans and eleven LM raters place four of the six in the two highest bins (\S\ref{sec:metric}--\ref{sec:census}). (ii)~A controlled KG-swap protocol on four benchmarks with up to four graph retrievers, including HippoRAG~2 run from its released code on three of them, carried to answer accuracy with two readers (four on 2WikiMultiHop; \S\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}). (iii)~A mechanism: the inflation is carried by the gold's own structure (its gold-precise entity-to-passage index and, for PPR-based retrievers, its evidence edges), not by graph reasoning over an independently built structure or by training exposure. On 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA, a gold-source structure not induced from the evaluated questions inflates only pure PPR, by at most $0.105$ hit@5 (\S\ref{sec:results}). (iv)~A released evaluation suite that also scores each retriever with a gold-blind KG, and a reporting protocol (\S\ref{sec:recommend}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Graph-based and KG-grounded retrieval.} A growing number of systems build a graph or KB over the corpus and retrieve over it~\citep{edge2024graphrag,gutierrez2024hipporag,gutierrez2025hipporag2}. Several benchmarks in our sample are constructed from a KG or KB. \citet{graphragbench2025} and \citet{han2025ragvsgraphrag} show that the benefit of structure depends on the task. Our question is orthogonal: we measure where the gold came from, not task difficulty.

\paragraph{Multi-hop benchmark reliability.} A mature line of work identifies answer-side shortcuts and disconnected reasoning in multi-hop QA~\citep{min2019compositional,trivedi2020dire}. This work motivated constructions such as MuSiQue with explicit anti-shortcut and leakage controls~\citep{trivedi2022musique}. These analyses target the answer side; reporting the provenance of retrieval gold has not been standardized. Answer-string distant supervision is a documented source of noisy evidence gold in open-domain QA~\citep{chen2017drqa,lin2018denoising}. Our rubric scores it as answer-surface derivation and separates it from KG derivation, which specifically favors graph retrievers.

\paragraph{Evaluation circularity and contamination.} Selecting and testing with the same data inflates results~\citep{kriegeskorte2009circular,kapoor2023leakage}. \citet{paulheim2017refinement} warns that using the same KG as the evaluation standard can overrate KG-refinement methods adapted to its coverage bias. LM judges favor models trained on data from a related generator~\citep{li2026preferenceleakage}. We transfer this concern to retrieval gold in knowledge-grounded QA and operationalize it per scored axis. Recent studies examine annotation and question-quality defects in KG question answering~\citep{zhang2025kgqapitfalls}, question--corpus mismatch and LM judge bias~\citep{judgebias2025}, or a recognition-versus-reasoning bottleneck in multi-hop QA~\citep{weakestlink2026}. A benchmark can avoid all three yet derive its retrieval gold from the KG under test.

Training-data contamination~\citep{sainz2023contamination,golchin2024timetravel,deng2024contamination} is a system-side property: whether an evaluated model saw the benchmark, its corpus, or its KG, including test--train overlap in open-domain QA~\citep{lewis2021qaoverlap}. Provenance circularity is benchmark-side and fixed at construction time. Adjacent KG retrieval evaluations test reasoning under incomplete knowledge~\citep{zhou2025brink} or generate question-specific ground-truth subgraphs~\citep{cattaneo2026gtsubgraphs}. None audits the provenance of retrieval gold per axis across benchmarks.

\section{Provenance Circularity}
\label{sec:metric}
A benchmark $\bench$ provides questions and, for each, gold supporting units such as passages, sentences, entities, or edges on one or more gold-unit axes $a$, with $U_{\bench,a}$ the units on axis $a$. Let $\kg$ be a KG built from the corpus and $A$ the answer surface, the spans in which the answer string appears. For a unit $u$, let $\mathrm{circ}_{s}(u)\in[0,1]$ be the weight a construction trace attributes to source $s\in\{\kg,A\}$: $1$ when $u$ was copied from $s$ (or, for $A$, selected solely because it contains the answer string) and $0$ when it was built independently of $s$ (intermediate weights in Appendix~\ref{app:defdetails}). The axis score is
\begin{equation}
\C_a(\bench) = \frac{1}{|U_{\bench,a}|}\sum_{u\in U_{\bench,a}} \max_{s}\,\mathrm{circ}_{s}(u).
\label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
We report $\C_a$ per axis, because a benchmark can be independent on one axis and circular on another. We also keep the KG component $\C_{a,\kg}$ separate from the answer-surface component: gold derived from $\kg$ can favor a retriever that ranks over $\kg$, whereas gold derived from $A$ inflates any retriever that matches the answer string. Every graph-retrieval claim below rests on the KG component. No retriever quantity enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:C}, so $\C_a\in[0,1]$ is a benchmark-side property, fixed before any system runs or is trained.

\section{A Census of Knowledge-Grounded QA Benchmarks}
\label{sec:census}
\paragraph{Rubric and raters.} Per-unit provenance traces are rarely released, so we estimate $\C_a$ from each benchmark's published construction description. The rubric (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:rubric}; full text in Appendix~\ref{app:rubricfull}) maps a description to five ordinal bins, $\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}$: independent of $\kg$ and $A$ (bin~0), selected under $\kg$ or $A$ guidance (1), derived and then validated (2), generated from $\kg$ or retained by answer-string distant supervision (3), and copied from $\kg$ or selected by the answer string alone (4). One-step corrections apply for KG augmentation, human refinement, and anti-leakage filtering. The census conclusion rests on the binary reading, derived (bin~2 or above) versus independent; the bins record degree. We write $\Chat_a$ for this estimate, $\Chat_{\kg}$ for its maximum over KG-derived retrieval-unit axes (Appendix~\ref{app:fullcensus}), and $\Chat$ over all axes. Every bin carries a verbatim construction quote (Appendix~\ref{app:quotes}).

Two LM raters, blind to each other, applied the full rubric to each benchmark's paper for $29$ axis-level judgments over eleven benchmarks and two calibration anchors of our own. The upper anchor's gold entities, edges, and paths are copied from a KG by code; the lower anchor is a small manufacturing-domain benchmark we built from source text (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). One-bin disagreements were resolved to the lower bin; larger ones would have gone to a third rater, but none arose. The raters agreed exactly on $25$ of $29$ (quadratically weighted $\kappa=0.977$, ordinal Krippendorff's $\alpha=0.938$~\citep{krippendorff2004}; Appendix~\ref{app:confusion}).

\paragraph{Evidence behind the bins.} Table~\ref{tab:census} marks the strongest evidence behind each benchmark's bins. For 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, WebQSP, and our two anchors, the axis that sets $\Chat_{\kg}$ is exact: the released gold is a set of KG triples, is read from the KB by construction, or is written by our code (E). For GraphRAG-Bench, GRADE, and STaRK we traced the construction to the papers, code, repositories, and released data, where available. Every trace confirms the KG-derived reading; for GraphRAG-Bench it leaves the degree between bins~2 and~3 (T; Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}). Only the five text-built benchmarks rest on the construction description and its verbatim quote alone (D; Appendix~\ref{app:quotes}); HotpotQA and MuSiQue were checked on sampled units (Appendix~\ref{app:llmpanel}).

\paragraph{Human and LM raters.} Three PhD students in AI or data science from one lab, not involved in the project, each rated all $29$ judgments alone and without AI assistance under a pre-specified protocol. They applied the rubric's step procedure to verbatim construction excerpts and saw neither the census bins nor our short per-axis summaries of each construction (condensed descriptions; Appendix~\ref{app:descriptions}). They agree with one another at ordinal $\alpha=0.948$ ($95\%$ confidence interval (CI) $[0.843,1.000]$) and with the census at $\kappa=0.816$--$0.875$. A fourth non-author, given a short rater prompt (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}) and the condensed descriptions, agrees with the census at $\kappa=0.908$.

Four LMs applied the step procedure in logged runs. They agree with the census at $\kappa=0.789$--$0.965$ and with the median human at $0.79$--$0.88$. None passes the alternative annotator test~\citep{calderon2025alttest} at its pre-specified margin, so we use LM raters as robustness checks (Appendices~\ref{app:humanraters} and~\ref{app:llmpanel}).

Five more LMs scored only the axis that attains each benchmark's $\Chat$. Under each of the fifteen individual raters, GRADE, 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and WebQSP stay at $\Chat_{\kg}\geq0.75$; $13$ of the fifteen place STaRK there, and $11$ place GraphRAG-Bench on the derived side, as its construction record does (Appendix~\ref{app:raterrobust}). The causal results of \S\ref{sec:results} use no census rater.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:census} reports the census. The dominant pattern is axis dependence. MuSiQue~\citep{trivedi2022musique}, HotpotQA~\citep{yang2018hotpotqa}, and MultiHop-RAG~\citep{tang2024multihoprag}, whose gold is grounded in source text by human annotation or source-faithful composition, have $\Chat_a=0$ on every axis.

Several widely used benchmarks are circular precisely on the axes used to score graph methods. The evidence in 2WikiMultiHop~\citep{ho2020twowiki} is a set of Wikidata triples ($\C_a=1.0$ on the edge axis, exact); MetaQA~\citep{zhang2018metaqa} generates questions and answers from a movie KB through templates; WebQSP~\citep{yih2016webqsp} reads answer entities from Freebase by executing the gold parse. STaRK~\citep{wu2024stark} selects answer entities by relation traversal, and GRADE~\citep{lee2025grade} enumerates paths in an extracted KG. GraphRAG-Bench~\citep{graphragbench2025} builds a KG from its corpus with an LM and generates its questions from that KG's subgraphs and paths, as its construction record confirms (Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}). For MetaQA and WebQSP, retrieval is itself KB lookup; we retain both because graph-versus-lexical conclusions are drawn from them.

TriviaQA~\citep{joshi2017triviaqa} illustrates the answer-surface mechanism: answer-string matching designates its evidence documents, giving $\Chat_a=0.75$ on that axis but $\Chat_{\kg}=0$. Natural Questions~\citep{kwiatkowski2019nq} likewise has $\Chat_{\kg}=0$.

Circularity thus concentrates on the entity, edge, and path axes on which graph methods are scored (RQ1). The published record co-varies with it: across $28$ same-table graph-versus-sparse comparisons from seven papers, median gains rise with $\Chat_{\kg}$ (descriptive Spearman $\rho=0.86$ over eleven benchmark medians, STaRK's three subsets counted separately; Appendix~\ref{app:scatter}).

\section{Measuring the Consequence: A Controlled KG Swap}
\label{sec:setup}
The census shows that circular gold is common. To measure what it does to a graph-over-lexical comparison, we hold every component fixed except the KG that the graph retriever ranks over (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}).

\paragraph{Benchmarks and metric.} We use the four audited benchmarks that release a text corpus (for STaRK-Prime, entity documents) and the structured evidence their gold was derived from: 2WikiMultiHop (Wikidata evidence triples; $\C_a=1.0$ on the edge axis, exact), MetaQA (movie KB; $\C_a=1.0$ on the answer and path axes, exact), GraphRAG-Bench (released evidence relations; $\Chat_{\kg}=0.75$, KG-derived by its construction record), and STaRK-Prime (biomedical KB; $\Chat_{\kg}=0.75$). WebQSP has no text corpus, and GRADE releases its generation code but not its data. From each we draw two disjoint $200$-question splits with a fixed seed, a first and a held-out split. On 2WikiMultiHop both hold inference-type two-hop questions, and a third split of compositional questions tests a second question type. The candidate pool is the split's corpus (on 2WikiMultiHop, the questions' context paragraphs; $1{,}155$--$1{,}584$ passages). A question counts toward hit@5 only when all of its gold passages (two on 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA) rank in the top five (Appendix~\ref{app:swaprobust}).

\paragraph{KG conditions.} The \emph{aligned} KG is the benchmark's own gold-source structure restricted to the evaluated questions (on STaRK-Prime it covers the whole corpus): the Wikidata evidence triples of 2WikiMultiHop, the KB paths of MetaQA, and the KB subgraph of STaRK-Prime on its corpus. GraphRAG-Bench does not release its ontology graph, so its aligned KG is extracted from its released evidence relations, the triples of that graph from which its questions were generated (Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}). The \emph{independent} KG is an open-information-extraction (OpenIE) KG built by Llama-3.1-8B over the whole corpus with no access to questions, gold, or the aligned KG. Two realistic controls replace the aligned arm by structures not induced from the evaluated questions: the full MetaQA KB, and a KG fetched from live Wikidata for the 2WikiMultiHop corpus entities, of which $86$--$88\%$ resolve (Appendix~\ref{app:concentration}).

\paragraph{Retrievers.} BM25~\citep{robertson2009bm25} is the fixed, provenance-free reference. The graph retrievers rank passages through the KG's node-to-passage links, which the aligned KG inherits from the gold's source structure and the OpenIE KG gets from extraction. \emph{PPR} runs personalized PageRank from question-matched nodes, in the manner of HippoRAG~\citep{gutierrez2024hipporag}. The \emph{hybrid} retriever fuses three channels by reciprocal-rank fusion~\citep{cormack2009rrf}: BM25, a dense channel, and a KG channel that ranks passages linked from question-matched nodes. When those nodes cover less than half of the question's tokens, it adds a two-hop KG-recall channel. \emph{Hybrid-PPR} is the same retriever with PPR as its KG channel. PPR and hybrid-PPR are the PPR-based retrievers; the hybrid and hybrid-PPR retrievers are the hybrids. \emph{HippoRAG~2}~\citep{gutierrez2025hipporag2} is run from its released code with a smaller embedding model and a local LM, and each KG replaces HippoRAG~2's own extraction (Appendix~\ref{app:hipporag2}). In the main runs the hybrids' dense channel is a lexical surrogate (word and character $n$-gram TF--IDF); a BGE-M3 dense channel reproduces the pattern (Appendix~\ref{app:swaprobust}).

\paragraph{Readers and statistics.} For answer accuracy, the top five passages of each retrieval condition are given in one fixed prompt to Llama-3.1-8B~\citep{grattafiori2024llama3} and Qwen2.5-14B~\citep{qwen2025qwen25} at temperature zero. On 2WikiMultiHop the passages also go to a larger third reader, Mistral-Small-24B (temperature zero), and to the proprietary Claude Sonnet~5.5 (default sampling). Answers are scored by alias-aware exact match (EM). GraphRAG-Bench, whose gold answers are sentences, is scored by a Mistral-Small-24B judge, which agrees with four blind human raters at $\kappa=0.66$--$0.80$ on $100$ sampled verdicts (Appendix~\ref{app:graphragbench}). Closed-book and gold-passage-only conditions bound each reader. All comparisons are paired per question, with $10{,}000$-resample bootstrap $95\%$ CIs, exact two-sided sign tests, and a Holm correction~\citep{holm1979} over the $48$-cell answer grid.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\paragraph{An example.} For ``Who is Walter Long (Died 1610)'s paternal grandfather?'', the gold passages are the pages of Walter Long and his father Robert Long. The released evidence is the triples (Walter Long, father, Robert Long) and (Robert Long, father, Henry Long). BM25 finds Walter Long's page but not Robert Long's, whose name the question never states. With the aligned KG, PPR starts at the Walter Long node, walks the \emph{father} edge, and returns only the two gold pages, since no other linked passage is reachable from that node. With the independent KG it returns five other pages.
\paragraph{Alignment inflates retrieval.} On 2WikiMultiHop the aligned KG raises the hybrid retriever's hit@5 by a paired $0.160$ on the first split (CI $[0.100,0.220]$, $p=4.4\times10^{-7}$) and $0.115$ on the held-out split ($p=6.1\times10^{-4}$). Of the $37$ questions it flips from miss to hit, $29$ are BM25 misses, so alignment produces the beyond-lexical wins credited to the graph channel. PPR and the hybrid-PPR retriever, which rely more on the KG, show larger swaps ($0.315$ and $0.355$ on the first split, $0.310$ and $0.335$ held-out). HippoRAG~2, run with its default retrieval pipeline, shows $+0.240$ and $+0.275$ (sign test $p=2.4\times10^{-9}$ and $4.9\times10^{-13}$), $+0.180$ and $+0.170$ on MetaQA, and $+0.265$ and $+0.225$ on GraphRAG-Bench (all $p<10^{-5}$; Appendix~\ref{app:hipporag2}).

The compositional split replicates the swap for all four retrievers ($+0.100$ to $+0.615$, $p<10^{-4}$) and, for the two PPR-based retrievers, its EM swap with both readers (Appendix~\ref{app:swaprobust}). The swap replicates in all six retriever--split cells of MetaQA (five significant) and of GraphRAG-Bench (all significant) and attenuates on STaRK-Prime (one of six significant; Appendices~\ref{app:metaqa}--\ref{app:graphragbench}).

\paragraph{The inflation reaches answers where readers have headroom.} On 2WikiMultiHop the aligned KG raises EM in all twelve retriever--split--reader cells, significantly in ten before and seven after Holm correction. The gains track the retrieval swap: $+0.045$ to $+0.065$ for the hybrid retriever, $+0.100$ to $+0.170$ for the hybrid-PPR retriever, and $+0.260$ to $+0.345$ for PPR (Table~\ref{tab:e2e}). The larger third reader shows a significant swap in all eight 2WikiMultiHop cells, and Claude Sonnet~5.5 in seven of eight (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:thirdreader}). EM rises by $+0.24$ to $+0.60$ on questions whose hit@5 flips from miss to hit and, for the hybrids, moves within $\pm0.07$ on the rest (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:mediation}).

On GraphRAG-Bench, judge accuracy rises for the two PPR-based retrievers ($+0.040$ to $+0.220$, four of eight cells significant before and two after correction) but not significantly for the hybrid retriever, whose retrieval swap is small. There, PPR with the independent KG leaves the readers below closed-book accuracy, so its swap mixes inflation with distractor harm. HippoRAG~2's answer swap is small ($+0.020$ to $+0.075$ EM on 2WikiMultiHop with the two smaller readers; Appendix~\ref{app:hipporag2}). On MetaQA and STaRK-Prime, answer accuracy does not move (one uncorrected cell of $24$ is significant).

These nulls fit the headroom pattern. Across the $100$ benchmark--split--retriever--reader cells of the open-weight readers, including the BGE-M3 runs, HippoRAG~2, and the third reader, the answer swap follows the retrieval swap scaled by the reader's headroom, gold-only minus BM25-passage accuracy floored at zero ($R^2=0.56$ and Spearman $0.78$, against $R^2=0.45$ for the retrieval swap alone; Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:cells}). STaRK-Prime's retrieval swap averages $0.02$, and on MetaQA BM25 already retrieves both gold documents for $0.86$ of the questions. The fit predicts mean answer swaps of $+0.014$ and $+0.004$ there; we observe $+0.000$ and $+0.004$ (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:cells}).

\paragraph{What carries the inflation.} Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} traces the inflation to two parts of the gold's own structure. The first is the entity-to-passage index. The aligned KG links $342$ of the $1{,}256$ passages, every one a gold passage, whereas the OpenIE KG links $1{,}251$ at a gold fraction of $31\%$ (MetaQA and GraphRAG-Bench: $31\%$ and $13\%$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:linkcounts}). For the hybrid retriever, which ranks passages linked from the nodes a question names, the index suffices. Deleting every edge leaves its advantage ($+0.130$; $+0.075$ held-out), and diluting the index barely matters because dilution adds links but removes none. Adding the aligned nodes to the OpenIE graph transfers part of the gain only while they keep their gold links (significant on the first split).

The second part is the edges, which on 2WikiMultiHop are the gold evidence triples themselves. The PPR-based retrievers reach the second gold passage by walking them. Deleting the edges therefore collapses PPR and returns the hybrid-PPR retriever to the independent level, and diluting the index removes the diffusion-based PPR's inflation.

Restricting the independent KG's passage links to gold passages creates inflation for all three retrievers, although its nodes and edges were extracted without gold access. On the aligned KG, what looks like multi-hop graph retrieval is a lookup in, or a walk along, the gold's own structure. Realistic structures (last two rows) inflate only PPR, modestly ($+0.060$ and $+0.070$ on MetaQA, $+0.105$ on the held-out 2WikiMultiHop split), and raise no answer cell significantly by the sign test (Appendix~\ref{app:concentration}).

\paragraph{Training exposure does not explain it.} Retriever configuration and reader are identical across arms, so what a model saw in training is held constant by design; we also measure it. Asked closed-book for the object of each of the $800$ evidence-triple instances of the $400$ 2WikiMultiHop questions, Llama-3.1-8B recalls $0.161$ of them and Qwen2.5-14B $0.154$. Closed-book, the readers answer $0.015$--$0.175$ of the 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and STaRK-Prime questions (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:readerref}).

Table~\ref{tab:exposure} restricts the swap to the questions whose answer the reader cannot give closed-book and none of whose evidence triples it recalls ($277$ and $295$ of $400$). On this subset the swap keeps its size for every retriever; only the hybrid retriever's small effect loses significance for Qwen2.5-14B in the smaller sample. On GraphRAG-Bench the PPR swap is larger on the questions the reader cannot answer closed-book than on those it can.

On 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA the OpenIE extractor reproduces no gold triple whose object is absent from its source passage (Appendix~\ref{app:memorization}). Denying the system access to $\kg$ is necessary but not sufficient: the OpenIE KG still contains $73$--$81\%$ of the aligned KG's nodes, because corpus and gold describe the same entities (Appendix~\ref{app:systemside}).

\paragraph{KG, retriever, and reader quality.} The swap changes only the KG. We ask whether the quality of the KG, of the retriever, or of the reader, rather than alignment, could explain the effect. The aligned KG is correct gold structure and the independent KG is extracted automatically, so the aligned arm's advantage could reflect KG quality. Two controls in Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} separate KG quality from alignment. Restricting the OpenIE KG's passage links to gold passages creates inflation for all three of our retrievers ($+0.110$, $+0.215$, and $+0.300$ hit@5 on the first 2WikiMultiHop split), although its nodes and edges were extracted without gold access. Live Wikidata and the full MetaQA KB come from the gold's own sources but are not induced from the evaluated questions, and they inflate only pure PPR, by at most $0.105$ hit@5 (Appendix~\ref{app:concentration}). For the retriever, reliance on the graph scales the effect. Hybrid-PPR shares the hybrid retriever's matcher and index yet gains about two to five times as much on 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and GraphRAG-Bench (Appendix~\ref{app:indexprec}), and the published HippoRAG~2 system also shows the swap. For the reader, the answer swap follows the retrieval swap scaled by the reader's headroom. The readers range from Llama-3.1-8B to the proprietary Claude Sonnet~5.5, which shows the answer swap in all eight 2WikiMultiHop cells, significantly in seven (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:thirdreader}).

Graph retrievers beat BM25 significantly in $24$ of $30$ benchmark--split--retriever cells with the aligned structure and in $5$ with the independent KG ($20$ and $3$ with Holm correction per arm); $19$ verdicts flip, $5$ of them to significantly below BM25, and none flips the other way. Realistic structures fall between the arms: on the $16$ 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA cells, $15$ beat BM25 with the aligned KG, $6$ with live Wikidata or the full KB (the two extra wins are PPR on 2WikiMultiHop), and $4$ with the independent KG. Of the $30$ published pairs we audit (two with a dense baseline), $11$ rank over the benchmark's source KB, $17$ over a graph built from the corpus, two over Wikipedia and Wikidata, and none over the questions' released evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:scatter}). The aligned arm is an upper bound, not published practice. At the answer level the count falls from $9$ to $4$ of $30$ cells with Qwen2.5-14B and from $12$ to $5$ with Llama-3.1-8B (Appendix~\ref{app:e2edetail}). On the first 2WikiMultiHop split our graph retrievers' EM advantage over BM25 falls from $0.07$--$0.36$ to at most $0.02$ with Qwen2.5-14B.

The independent arm also preserves gains that do not need the aligned KG. HippoRAG~2 normally ranks over a self-extracted graph, as in our independent arm, and there it exceeds its dense channel on 2WikiMultiHop by $0.150$ and $0.175$ ($p<10^{-4}$; $0.390$ and $0.450$ with the aligned KG). On 2WikiMultiHop our hybrids with a BGE-M3 dense channel exceed that channel by $0.10$--$0.27$ with the aligned KG and by at most $0.02$ with the independent one (Appendix~\ref{app:swaprobust}). On GraphRAG-Bench, HippoRAG~2's retrieval gain over its dense channel comes almost entirely from the aligned KG, and its judge-accuracy swap is small ($-0.010$ to $+0.040$; Appendix~\ref{app:hipporag2}). Where the aligned arm is the released evidence structure, most of the graph advantage we measure depends on that structure's provenance, not on graph retrieval as such (RQ2, RQ3).

\section{Recommendations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:recommend}
Three practices follow. Benchmarks should report exact $\C_a$ when per-unit traces exist and $\Chat_a$ otherwise, and release source-attributed traces. Claims of graph superiority should be made on axes with $\C_a=0$, or come with a de-circularized column that scores the same retriever against a KG built without reference to the gold. Systems should state whether any structure they ingest or component they trained derives from the benchmark's gold source.

Withholding that structure is the first remedy and works for the hybrid retrievers here, but is not complete. A realistic structure still inflates pure PPR, passage links chosen with gold access inflate even an independently extracted KG, and on MetaQA and WebQSP retrieval is KB lookup by construction.

$\C_a$ flags where inflation is possible, not how large it is. Within 2WikiMultiHop the swap tracks how precisely the structure links the question's entities to gold passages (Spearman $0.45$--$0.82$ by retriever over $12$--$13$ KG variants); across benchmarks the reader's headroom, not the census value, shapes the answer swap (Appendices~\ref{app:e2edetail} and~\ref{app:indexprec}). The de-circularized column, not the census value, is the measurement to report. We release the census, its quotes, and an evaluation suite with all KGs, retrieval results, and reader outputs.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Vitamin-Village/retrieval-gold-provenance}}

Six of eleven benchmarks (four to six by rater) have KG-derived gold at $\Chat_{\kg}\geq0.75$ (RQ1); gold alignment inflates hit@5 and, where readers have headroom, answers (RQ2); the gold's passage links and edges carry it, and an independent KG removes most of the graph-over-lexical margin (RQ3).

\section*{Limitations}
\paragraph{Provenance estimation.} The census estimates circularity from published construction descriptions, because most benchmarks do not release source-attributed per-unit traces. A methodology-level bin $\Chat_a$ is a proxy for the per-unit average $\C_a$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:C}. Traces with binary lineage or attribution weights would allow exact computation. Where per-unit provenance is recoverable the estimate matches it at the endpoints: the 2WikiMultiHop edge axis and the upper anchor's KG axes are copied from a KG by code ($\C_{a}=1.0$), MetaQA and WebQSP answers are read from the KB, and the lower anchor is text-only ($\C_a=0$). The intermediate bins rest on construction descriptions. Per-unit checks are limited to the lexical rules of Appendix~\ref{app:perunit} (which diagnose surface realization rather than the subgraph itself), the STaRK template re-execution (Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}), and logged LM readings of $130$ units (Appendix~\ref{app:llmpanel}). For GraphRAG-Bench, GRADE, and STaRK the construction record fixes the derivation order (Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}). The score applies to benchmarks whose supporting gold is scored by overlap; win-rate and gold-free evaluations are out of scope.

\paragraph{Rater reliability.} The census bins come from two LM raters whose model identities were not recorded; both raters' bins, their agreement, and rater A's quote records are released. Four non-author humans and nine further LM raters check them. The three humans of the main check are PhD students from one lab recruited by the authors. They rated separately, but raters who share training and read the same excerpts may share a framing. With $29$ judgments, the alternative annotator test has little power. The condensed descriptions move a human reading toward the census: the human who saw them agrees with it at $\kappa=0.908$ but with the three excerpt-only humans at $0.66$--$0.73$. We therefore rest the census conclusion on the binary derived-versus-independent reading, which is exact or traced to primary sources for every KG-constructed benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:census}). We report every quote (Appendix~\ref{app:quotes}) and every human departure from the census (Appendix~\ref{app:humanraters}), and release every human bin. The degree between adjacent bins is a rubric reading.

\paragraph{Causal scope.} The swaps cover the four audited benchmarks that release both a corpus and the structured evidence their gold was derived from. WebQSP has no corpus and GRADE no released data; the five text-built benchmarks have no KG-derived gold to swap. Two swaps are at maximal circularity (2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA) and two at a non-maximal estimate (GraphRAG-Bench, STaRK-Prime). The aligned arms of 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and GraphRAG-Bench are induced from the evaluated questions' released evidence, so they represent a system that ingests the benchmark's evidence structure. GraphRAG-Bench's arm approximates its unreleased ontology subgraph. On 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA, structures that are not induced from the questions inflate only pure PPR, by at most $0.105$ hit@5 (Appendix~\ref{app:concentration}); STaRK-Prime's whole-corpus aligned KB inflates hybrid-PPR in one held-out cell ($+0.065$). The live-Wikidata KG and the full MetaQA KB both come from the gold's own sources. Whether an unrelated KB behaves like the OpenIE arm is untested. STaRK-Prime attenuates: one of six cells is significant on the full set, and a post hoc, uncorrected analysis of the questions BM25 misses finds the swap positive in all six cells and significant in three (Appendix~\ref{app:stark}). On 2WikiMultiHop the swap covers inference and compositional questions; comparison and bridge-comparison questions are untested. We claim controlled inflation mediated by the gold's own structure (its gold-derived passage links and, for the PPR-based retrievers, its evidence edges), not a dose--response indexed by the census value.

\paragraph{Retrievers, readers, and judges.} Three of the four graph retrievers are our implementations; HippoRAG~2 is run with a smaller embedding model than its default and with Llama-3.1-8B as its language model (Appendix~\ref{app:hipporag2}). The main hybrid runs use a lexical surrogate for the dense channel; a BGE-M3 dense channel reproduces the pattern. We measure the end-to-end effect with two open-weight readers, plus a larger third reader and a proprietary reader on 2WikiMultiHop, under one fixed prompt. The two main readers agree on direction in every 2WikiMultiHop and GraphRAG-Bench cell of Table~\ref{tab:e2e} and on significance in all but two GraphRAG-Bench cells there. Mistral-Small-24B shows the effect in every 2WikiMultiHop cell and Claude Sonnet~5.5 in all eight, significantly in seven, but both ran on 2WikiMultiHop only. GraphRAG-Bench answers depend on an LM judge~\citep{zheng2023judging}. Four human raters checked $100$ of its verdicts. One rater, under a short rule, agrees with it on $86$ ($\kappa=0.72$) and is more lenient. The other three, under a guide with explicit rules for incorrect answers, agree on $82$--$90$ ($\kappa=0.66$--$0.80$) and are stricter, as are three Claude models under that guide. The judge thus lies between the two readings, and it applies one criterion to both arms.

\paragraph{Training exposure.} Training exposure is held constant across swap arms and measured for the readers through closed-book answering and evidence-triple recall (Appendix~\ref{app:memorization}). The OpenIE extractor, Llama-3.1-8B, has likely seen Wikipedia in pretraining. None of the $233$ OpenIE edges that reproduce a 2WikiMultiHop gold triple has an object missing from its source passage (none of $976$ on MetaQA), so we find no gold fact the extractor could only have recalled. The test cannot detect memory aiding extraction within a passage (Appendix~\ref{app:memorization}); any such alignment makes the independent arm more aligned and the swap estimate conservative. Training exposure is not measured for HippoRAG~2's embedding model; its filter LM is Llama-3.1-8B, measured above, and both are identical across arms. Recall probes bound, but do not exhaust, what a model has seen, so contamination tests remain complementary to the benchmark-side audit.

\paragraph{Lower-anchor benchmark.} Our lower-anchor benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}) is small, spans four manufacturing domains in English, and has near-ceiling lexical retrieval (BM25 hit@5 $0.94$); it is a $\C_a=0$ construction template and calibration anchor, not an evaluation of graph retrieval.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
This work audits the construction of public benchmarks using only released metadata and published descriptions, and it does not target individuals. The audit aims to make evaluation contracts explicit rather than to disqualify any benchmark. All human raters are non-authors who volunteered without payment. They rated published construction text or model answers only, and the released ratings carry their field and experience but not their names. We used language models as census raters (the two primary raters; Claude Opus~5.5, Sonnet~5.5, and Haiku~4.5; Gemini~3.8 Flash; and the five cross-model raters of Appendix~\ref{app:crossmodel}), as the OpenIE extractor and readers (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-Small-24B; Claude Sonnet~5.5), as the answer judge and its checks (Mistral-Small-24B; Qwen2.5-14B and Llama-3.1-8B as cross-judges; Claude Opus~5, Sonnet~5, and Haiku~4.5), and as the generators, verifiers, and spot-check rater of the lower-anchor benchmark (Mistral-Small-24B and Claude Sonnet; Gemini~2.5 Flash and Qwen2.5-14B; Claude Haiku~4.5; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Local models ran on one consumer GPU (RTX~3090). The logged census raters, the LM judge check, and the proprietary reader made $7{,}015$ API requests that returned a reply.

\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, label=box:rubric, title=\textbf{Provenance-circularity scoring rubric, applied per gold-unit axis},
  colback=gray!3, colframe=gray!55, colbacktitle=black!75, coltitle=white, fonttitle=\small\bfseries, boxrule=0.6pt]
\footnotesize
\textbf{Step 0, gate.} The benchmark must have supporting gold that is scored by overlap such as hit@$k$ or F1; win-rate and gold-free benchmarks are out of scope.\\
\textbf{Step 1, axes.} Score every available gold-unit axis separately and report all axes: passage or sentence; answer; and entity, edge, or path.\\
\textbf{Step 2, bins.} For each axis, classify how the gold was produced by derivation from the KG $\kg$ or from the answer surface $A$:\\
\hspace*{1em}\textbf{bin 4}, $\Chat_a{=}1.0$: gold copied directly from the evaluation KG $\kg$, or selected solely because it contains the answer string;\\
\hspace*{1em}\textbf{bin 3}, $\Chat_a{=}0.75$: auto-generated from KG triples or templates, or evidence retained by answer-string distant supervision;\\
\hspace*{1em}\textbf{bin 2}, $\Chat_a{=}0.50$: gold derived from $\kg$ or $A$ by auto-generation and then human-validated;\\
\hspace*{1em}\textbf{bin 1}, $\Chat_a{=}0.25$: human or text gold with KG-guided or answer-guided selection;\\
\hspace*{1em}\textbf{bin 0}, $\Chat_a{=}0.0$: human or source-text gold, independent of $\kg$ and $A$.\\
\textbf{Step 3, corrections.} KG augmentation, human refinement, and anti-leakage filtering each shift the bin one step lower; these shifts are applied at most twice and never below bin 0.\\
\textbf{Step 4, evidence.} Attach a verbatim construction quote to each bin assignment.\\
\textbf{Steps 5--6, adjudication and agreement.} Resolve one-bin disagreements to the lower bin, and adjudicate larger disagreements with a third rater; report Krippendorff's $\alpha$ with ordinal weighting and quadratically weighted Cohen's $\kappa$.
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent The three excerpt-only human raters and the logged LM raters (Appendices~\ref{app:humanraters} and~\ref{app:llmpanel}) applied the \emph{step procedure}: Steps~2--3 of the rubric posed as three questions. These ask for the source of the gold ($\kg$, $A$, or neither), its base bin (the Step-2 bin before corrections), and the Step-3 corrections, which lower the base bin to the final bin.

\subsection{Definition Details}
\label{app:defdetails}
The per-source component score $\C_{a,s}(\bench)$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:C} with $\max_{s}\mathrm{circ}_{s}(u)$ replaced by $\mathrm{circ}_{s}(u)$. The KG-derived score is $\C_{\kg}(\bench)=\max_{a}\C_{a,\kg}(\bench)$ over the retrieval-unit axes $a$ (passages, sentences, entities, edges, paths, subgraphs, and the answer entities of KB question-answering benchmarks). The overall score is $\C(\bench)=\max_{a}\C_a(\bench)$ over all reported gold axes. Answer strings generated from $\kg$ are KG-derived but are not retrieval units, so they enter $\C$ and not $\C_{\kg}$. A unit-level construction trace may assign an intermediate weight $\mathrm{circ}_{s}(u)\in(0,1)$ when it explicitly records partial lineage, as when KG-derived content is mixed with independent human refinement. If a release does not record binary lineage or an explicit fractional attribution, $\mathrm{circ}_{s}(u)$ is a latent target rather than an observable value. In that case the ordinal estimator $\Chat_{a,s}$ of \S\ref{sec:census} approximates the axis average $\C_{a,s}$ from the published construction description. Because $\C_{\kg}$ and $\C$ are maxima over axes, one circular axis suffices to confound a comparison scored on that axis, while the per-axis report preserves the axes that remain provenance-independent.

\subsection{Rubric Design}
\label{app:rubricdesign}
The bins record the degree of derivation from $\kg$ or $A$ (\S\ref{sec:census}). In bin~4 the source fixes the unit entirely: the unit is copied from $\kg$, or selected solely because it contains the answer string. In bin~3 the unit is generated from $\kg$ triples or templates, or retained by answer-string distant supervision. In bin~2 humans validate generated gold, which places it one step below unvalidated generation. Validation can reject a unit but does not change where the unit came from, so the axis stays on the derived side. In bin~1 humans or source text supply the gold, and $\kg$ or $A$ guides only which units are selected. In bin~0 neither source is involved.

The bin values place the bins on the scale of $\C_a$ so that the endpoints agree with the exact score of Eq.~\eqref{eq:C}. An axis whose units are all copied from $\kg$ has $\C_a=1.0$, and an axis built independently of $\kg$ and $A$ has $\C_a=0$. Where per-unit provenance is recoverable, the census matches it at these endpoints (Limitations). The intermediate values are ordinal estimates from a construction description, not measured fractions of units, and the degree between adjacent bins is a rubric reading. The census conclusion therefore rests on the binary reading: derived (bin~2 or above) versus independent (bin~1 or below). For every KG-constructed benchmark, this reading is exact or traced to primary sources (Table~\ref{tab:census}). Because the bins are ordinal, agreement is reported with ordinal weights (Krippendorff's $\alpha$ and quadratically weighted Cohen's $\kappa$), and the comparison with published gains is a descriptive rank correlation (Appendix~\ref{app:scatter}).

Each axis is scored separately because a benchmark can be independent on one axis and circular on another (\S\ref{sec:metric}). The KG and answer-surface sources are kept apart because gold derived from $\kg$ can favor a retriever that ranks over $\kg$, whereas gold derived from $A$ inflates any retriever that matches the answer string. The Step-3 corrections only lower a bin, and one-bin disagreements between raters resolve to the lower bin, so neither rule can raise the estimate of circularity. Step~4 attaches a verbatim construction quote to every bin assignment, so each can be checked against its source (Appendix~\ref{app:quotes}).

\subsection{Terms}
\label{app:terms}
Each term below is given with the place where it is defined.
{\footnotesize
\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item[Provenance circularity.] Gold derived from the KG $\kg$ that the evaluated graph retriever ranks over, or from the answer surface $A$ (\S\ref{sec:intro}, \S\ref{sec:metric}).
\item[Gold unit, gold-unit axis.] A supporting unit the benchmark scores against, such as a passage, sentence, answer, entity, edge, path, or subgraph; an axis is one unit type, scored separately (\S\ref{sec:metric}).
\item[Answer surface $A$.] The spans in which the answer string appears (\S\ref{sec:metric}).
\item[$\C_a$, $\Chat_a$, $\Chat_{\kg}$, $\Chat$.] The exact per-axis score of Eq.~\eqref{eq:C}; its five-bin rubric estimate; the maximum of the estimate's KG component over retrieval-unit axes; and its maximum over all axes (\S\ref{sec:metric}, \S\ref{sec:census}, Appendix~\ref{app:defdetails}).
\item[Retrieval-unit axes.] Passages, sentences, entities, edges, paths, subgraphs, and the answer entities of KB question-answering benchmarks; answer strings are not retrieval units (Appendix~\ref{app:defdetails}).
\item[Derived versus independent.] The binary reading of a bin: bin~2 or above versus bin~1 or below (\S\ref{sec:census}).
\item[Calibration anchors.] Two benchmarks of our own. The upper anchor's gold entities, edges, and paths are copied from a KG by code; the lower anchor is built from source text (\S\ref{sec:census}, Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).
\item[Condensed descriptions.] Our short per-axis summaries of each construction, shown to some raters (\S\ref{sec:census}, Appendix~\ref{app:descriptions}).
\item[Step procedure.] Steps~2--3 of the rubric posed as three questions, applied by the three excerpt-only humans and the logged LM raters (Appendix~\ref{app:rubricfull}).
\item[Aligned KG.] The benchmark's gold-source structure restricted to the evaluated questions; on STaRK-Prime it covers the whole corpus (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\item[Independent KG.] An OpenIE KG built by Llama-3.1-8B over the whole corpus with no access to questions, gold, or the aligned KG (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\item[Realistic structure.] A structure not induced from the evaluated questions: the full MetaQA KB, or a KG fetched from live Wikidata for the 2WikiMultiHop corpus entities (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\item[PPR, hybrid, hybrid-PPR.] PPR (``pure PPR'') runs personalized PageRank from question-matched nodes. The hybrid retriever fuses BM25, a dense channel, and a KG channel; hybrid-PPR uses PPR as its KG channel. PPR and hybrid-PPR are the PPR-based retrievers, and the hybrid and hybrid-PPR retrievers are the hybrids (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\item[hit@5.] The share of questions whose gold passages all rank in the top five (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\item[Closed-book, gold-only.] The reader answers without passages, or from the gold passages alone; the two conditions bound each reader (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\item[Swap.] The paired per-question difference, aligned minus independent KG, in hit@5 (retrieval swap) or answer accuracy (answer swap), with everything else fixed (\S\ref{sec:setup}, Table~\ref{tab:protocol}).
\item[Headroom.] A reader's gold-only accuracy minus its accuracy with BM25 passages, floored at zero (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item[Gold fraction.] The share of the passages a structure links to that are gold passages (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}).
\item[Unknown questions.] Questions whose answer the reader cannot give closed-book and none of whose evidence triples it recalls (\S\ref{sec:results}, Table~\ref{tab:exposure}).
\item[De-circularized evaluation.] Scoring a graph retriever against a KG built without gold access as well as against the aligned structure, with BM25 fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}, \S\ref{sec:recommend}).
\item[Index precision.] How precisely the passages linked from the nodes a retriever matches in the question hit the gold passages, scored by F1 (Appendix~\ref{app:indexprec}).
\end{description}}

\subsection{Human Raters}
\label{app:humanraters}
Three non-author raters applied the step procedure (Appendix~\ref{app:rubricfull}) to the $29$ axis judgments under a protocol written before any workbook was generated or sent. Raters were eligible if they were not authors, had no part in the project, and had a background in NLP, IR, or knowledge graphs. The three are PhD students in AI or data science from one lab, with one to two years in NLP or IR. Each rated separately.

Each received the instructions of the logged LM study (the step procedure with its base-bin table and corrections, and six worked examples on invented benchmarks) and one workbook. The workbook held the six-item qualification quiz and the $29$ judgments plus two attention items, in an order drawn per rater. Each item showed the benchmark and axis names, a one-line neutral definition of the axis, and the verbatim excerpts of the excerpt-only LM condition with their source and page or line. No condensed description, item identifier, or census bin was shown. For each judgment a rater entered the source, base bin, corrections, final bin, and confidence, and quoted the phrase that decided the bin. Raters were asked to work alone, to use no AI assistant or language model, not to search the web, and not to discuss items. Workbooks were locked except for the rating cells.

Before any statistic was computed, a pre-specified script checked each returned workbook against hashes of the sent files, item texts, and locked cells. The lab's computers share one office-software user name, so workbook metadata do not distinguish the raters. The author who distributed and collected the workbooks confirmed that each rater filled in the workbook on a separate computer. Each rater attested in the workbook to rating alone, without AI assistance, and from the sent materials only. A rater's ratings were analyzed if the workbook passed the integrity checks and the attestations, if the rater scored at least four of six on the quiz and did not miss both attention items by two or more bins, and if the workbook was complete.

All three workbooks passed every check (five of six on the quiz each, both attention items at the expected bin) and took $94$, $107$, and $110$ minutes by the recorded start and end times. Table~\ref{tab:humanraters} gives agreement. The three humans agree with one another at ordinal $\alpha=0.948$ ($95\%$ item-bootstrap CI $[0.843,1.000]$), or $0.922$ ($[0.764,1.000]$) on the $23$ judgments that exclude our anchors. Each pair agrees exactly on $27$ judgments and differs by two or more bins on the other two. On each of the three judgments involved (GRADE passage, GraphRAG-Bench subgraph, and STaRK textual), one rater stands apart from the other two.

Each human matches $22$ census bins. All three depart from the census on the same four axes: the 2WikiMultiHop and GraphRAG-Bench answer axes ($3\to0$), read as text-derived; Natural Questions passage ($1\to0$); and TriviaQA evidence ($3\to4$), read as selected by the answer string alone. Their other departures are the three judgments on which they also differ among themselves. For $\Chat_{\kg}$, every human places GRADE and STaRK at bin~3 and 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and WebQSP at bin~4; the three place GraphRAG-Bench at bins $2$, $2$, and $0$. Under each human and under their median, five external benchmarks reach $\Chat_{\kg}\geq0.75$ (Table~\ref{tab:raterrobust}).

Humans~2 and~3 quoted their deciding phrases from the same excerpt sentence on $26$ of the $29$ judgments. Humans~1 and~2 did so on $21$, and Humans~1 and~3 on $18$, of the $24$ judgments on which Human~1's phrase could be located in an excerpt sentence. Counting Humans~2 and~3 as one rater leaves two raters who agree at $\kappa=0.907$ or $0.934$, and no conclusion changes.

Against the logged LM runs, each human's closest single run matches the human's source, base bin, and final bin on $24$--$26$ of the $29$ judgments. On the 2WikiMultiHop answer axis, all three humans give bin~0 where $22$ of the $24$ runs give bin~4. A human's deciding sentence coincides with that of an excerpt-only LM run on $0.549$--$0.587$ of judgments on average, against $0.815$ for two reruns of one model. The fourth human, who worked from the short rater prompt and the condensed descriptions (Appendix~\ref{app:descriptions}), agrees with the census at $\kappa=0.908$ but with the other three humans at $\kappa=0.66$--$0.73$.

With three humans we apply the alternative annotator test~\citep{calderon2025alttest} as pre-specified. Each human is left out in turn. A rater and the left-out human are scored by $-$RMSE against the two remaining humans, and a one-sided $t$-test asks whether the rater's advantage probability trails the human's by less than a margin $\varepsilon$. The three tests are corrected with the Benjamini--Yekutieli procedure~\citep{benjamini2001by} ($q=0.05$). A rater passes if it wins against at least two of the three humans.

At the pre-specified $\varepsilon=0.15$, and at $0.20$, neither the census nor any LM rater passes. At $\varepsilon=0.25$, Opus~5.5 and Haiku~4.5 (both conditions) and Sonnet~5.5 (excerpt-only) pass. At $0.30$, every logged model, primary rater A, and the census pass; primary rater B fails at every margin up to $0.30$. Constant raters, baselines that assign one fixed bin to every judgment, fail at every margin. On $0.80$--$0.91$ of judgments, the LM raters and the census are at least as close to the remaining humans as the left-out human is, against $0.10$--$0.55$ for constant raters.

With $29$ judgments the test has little power: Opus~5.5, in either condition, ties the humans on $25$ or $26$ judgments, trails on three, and still fails at $\varepsilon=0.15$. We therefore report LM raters as robustness checks and rest the human validation on the three humans themselves.

\subsection{Logged LM Raters}

\subsection{Per-Unit Intermediate-Bin Checks}
\label{app:perunit}
Two intermediate-level diagnostics complement the endpoint checks summarized in the Limitations. Each is computed deterministically from released per-question fields by a conservative lexical rule that credits no paraphrase. On the 2WikiMultiHop sentence axis, a supporting-fact sentence counts as a surface realization when its document title matches an evidence-triple subject and the triple object appears in the sentence. Under this rule the per-unit realization fraction is $0.76$ on the $400$ inference questions of the first and held-out 2WikiMultiHop splits, and $0.81$ when parenthetical disambiguators in document titles are stripped before matching. On the full development set ($12{,}576$ questions; disambiguators stripped, pooled over sentences) it is $1.00$ for comparison and bridge-comparison questions, $0.89$ for compositional questions, and $0.80$ for inference questions, the type of the first and held-out splits.

GraphRAG-Bench requires a different interpretation. Its released fields pair source relations with $8{,}175$ evidence sentences, so our lexical rule checks whether a majority of a source relation's content tokens appear in each sentence. This evidence-sentence diagnostic has a question mean of $0.63$ over the $2{,}062$ questions of the Medical subset. It ranges from $0.59$ to $0.71$ on Complex Reasoning, Contextual Summarize, and Fact Retrieval questions and falls to $0.24$ on Creative Generation. Among the $3{,}676$ matched sentences, $0.14$ are near-verbatim copies of the matched relation string: after lowercasing and removing punctuation, their token Jaccard similarity with it is at least $0.9$, or one string contains the other.

These units are textual realizations of the evidence, not the gold subgraphs themselves, so neither $0.63$ nor $0.14$ is a per-unit estimate of $\C_{\text{subgraph}}$. The subgraph bin~3 instead rests on the construction record: the gold relations are triples of a KG built from the corpus, and human confirmation lowers otherwise direct structure derivation by one rubric step. The lexical result is consistent with extensive rewording but is only an auxiliary surface check. The raters disagreed on this axis. Four of the fifteen individual raters (three LMs and one human) placed it below bin~2, the short-prompt human at bin~4, and all five cross-model LMs below bin~3. We therefore traced its construction to primary sources (Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}).

\subsection{Source Traces of GraphRAG-Bench, GRADE, and STaRK}
\label{app:grbtrace}
GraphRAG-Bench is the one benchmark whose $\Chat_{\kg}$ the raters placed on both sides, KG-derived or text-grounded. Because a construction description can be read either way, we traced the construction to primary sources: the paper's construction appendix and prompts~\citep{graphragbench2025}, the GraphRAG-Bench authors' responses in that paper's public review discussion, and the history of the benchmark repository.

The record fixes the derivation order: a KG is extracted from the corpus first, and questions and answers are generated from its triples. For its Novel subset the paper states that ``we first construct a knowledge graph from the corpus, then form logic chains using relevant triples from this graph'' and asks for answers ``uniquely supported by the input triplets''. Its Medical prompts ask for questions ``that require direct factual retrieval from the relations list''. The GraphRAG-Bench authors' responses describe GPT-4.1 extracting structured triples into an initial KG per document or disease section.

Construction scripts for the Novel subset, in an earlier state of the repository (commit 431e376), build a graph from the corpus text with an LM. They keep its five largest subgraphs and enumerate paths of at least five hops from its highest-degree nodes. For the Medical subset used in our swap, the repository history shows that the field first released as each question's evidence equals the current evidence-relations field for all $2{,}062$ questions. The sentence-level evidence field was added in a later commit, with every question and answer unchanged.

The released data agree. Only $126$ of $5{,}184$ relation strings ($0.024$) and $43$ of $8{,}175$ evidence sentences ($0.005$) occur verbatim in the corpus, so neither was copied from text. Of the $2{,}062$ answers, $498$ ($0.24$; $0.45$ of Fact Retrieval answers) equal a relation string. Co-occurrence of subject and object in the evidence sentence is $0.38$ over the $1{,}573$ subject--object pairs of the swap's aligned KG, which we extract from the relation strings of the two swap splits (Appendix~\ref{app:graphragbench}). This measure does not separate text-derived from KG-derived gold: 2WikiMultiHop, whose evidence is copied from Wikidata (exact bin~4), scores $0.29$ on the same test.

The paper's Check \& Correct step discards a question when none of its evidence triples can be inferred from the corpus, a check ``supported by advanced models combined with human checking for final confirmation''. This check verifies the triples against the text; it does not make their selection independent of the KG.

Under the rubric (Appendix~\ref{app:rubricfull}), gold relations selected from the construction KG and released unchanged have base bin~4. This documented check is the human refinement that lowers them one step, to the census bin~3 on the subgraph axis. If the LM-extracted relations are instead read as auto-generated gold (base bin~3), the same check gives bin~2. The record thus fixes the direction, KG-derived, and leaves the degree open between bins~2 and~3. Answers generated from those triples, rather than copied from them, have base bin~3, and the same check lowers them to bin~2 ($0.50$); the census assigns bin~3. Either value leaves $\Chat_{\kg}=\Chat=0.75$. A text-grounded reading (bin~0) is inconsistent with the record: the KG is extracted from text, but the gold is taken from the KG.

Three limits remain. The construction KG and the question-generation code are not released, so the trace fixes the derivation order but not per-unit weights. The construction scripts described above, since removed from the repository, cover only the Novel subset, and no Medical construction code is public. Those scripts name a different extraction model than the paper. Deterministic code for every number above is released.

\paragraph{GRADE.} GRADE releases its construction code but no data. The code builds a directed graph from the triple table that an LM extracted from rewritten news sentences. It enumerates all shortest paths between node pairs, reads every gold triple from a graph edge in its path-finding code, and keeps paths of two to five hops. The question generator is told not to add knowledge beyond the given triples, and the only later filter is an LM ambiguity check. Gold edges copied from the KG have base bin~4. The documented augmentation with mirrored edges, which copy the source row, is the one-step correction to the census bin~3 on the edge axis. The supporting chunks are chunks of the source articles looked up from the path's triples; they are given to the question generator as context and enter the retrieval-difficulty score. This triple-guided selection fits the census bin~1 on the passage axis. Without released data, per-unit shares cannot be computed.

\paragraph{STaRK.} STaRK releases its knowledge bases and, for each query, only the query text and answer identifiers, with no template or anchor metadata. Its paper samples a relational template, takes the entities that satisfy it in the KB as candidates, extracts textual properties from one candidate's document, and keeps the candidates that three LMs judge to satisfy them. Humans verify answers only for the $263$ human-written queries.

We recovered each query's anchor entities from its text and executed the paper's templates on the released KB. A null repeats each test on randomly drawn same-type answer sets of the same size (fixed seed). On STaRK-Prime, $0.856$ of the $10{,}863$ anchored queries (of $11{,}204$) reach all of their gold answers through a template branch or one hop (null $0.065$). On STaRK-MAG, $0.939$ of the $13{,}166$ anchored queries (of $13{,}323$) satisfy a full template (null $0.048$). On STaRK-Amazon, only $0.431$ of multi-answer sets have a KB neighbor common to all their answers (null $0.001$), and $2.7\%$ of gold answers have no KB edge, so part of answer-set membership is decided outside the released KB. Answers selected by KB relations and then filtered on text therefore fit the census bin~3 on the answer-entity axis rather than bin~4.

The query text matches the gold entity's own document. We rank each gold document against $2{,}000$ randomly drawn same-type documents by how many of the query's content words, without anchor names and template words, it contains. Its percentile rank is the share of those documents that contain at least as many (lower is closer). The median is $0.021$ on MAG, $0.055$ on Amazon, and $0.017$ for Amazon's seed answers. Seed answers are the answers whose documents supplied the queries' textual properties; Amazon's first data release identifies them. On STaRK-Prime, whose queries paraphrase the document by design, the median is $0.402$. The textual axis is therefore source text selected by the relational template, which fits the census bin~1.

\paragraph{What the published systems retrieved over.} We classified each of the $30$ pairs by the structure the graph or KG system ranks over. Eleven rank over the benchmark's own source KB (MoR and QA-GNN~\citep{yasunaga2021qagnn} over the STaRK knowledge bases; PullNet, GraftNet~\citep{sun2018graftnet}, and key-value memory networks~\citep{miller2016kvmemnn} over the MetaQA KB or Freebase), a setting that matches our realistic full-KB arm. Another $17$ rank over a graph the system built from the corpus (HippoRAG, HippoRAG~2, HopRAG, KG-CQR, and the GraphRAG-Bench baselines), a setting that matches our independent arm. Two rank over Wikipedia and Wikidata, and none over a graph induced from the evaluated questions' released evidence, as our aligned arm does. The aligned arm is therefore an upper bound on what a system can gain from provenance alignment, not a description of published practice.

On the high-circularity benchmarks the two main classes report similar median gains ($23.7$ and $23.4$ points, the latter including GraphRAG-Bench's two dense-baseline pairs). Within self-built graphs the gains still rise with the census value ($25.1$ points over the three sparse-baseline pairs on 2WikiMultiHop against $7.1$ at $\Chat_{\kg}=0$). This is consistent with the gold inventory that an OpenIE graph inherits from a corpus written from the gold's source (Appendix~\ref{app:systemside}).

One published ablation points the same way within a system: on a 1{,}000-question WebQSP subset, Think-on-Graph gains $7.6$ (ToG) and $8.4$ (ToG-R) Hits@1 when it reasons over Freebase rather than Wikidata~\citep{sun2024thinkongraph}. Its authors attribute this to WebQSP being constructed upon Freebase and to harder search in the larger Wikidata.

\section{Controlled Swap Details}
\subsection{KG Swap Robustness}
\label{app:swaprobust}
\paragraph{Controlled-swap protocol and primary result.} We measure the effect of provenance alignment on 2WikiMultiHop while holding questions, gold, passages, and the lexical baseline fixed. Only the KG over which the hybrid retriever ranks varies: the aligned KG, built from the same Wikidata evidence as the gold, versus an OpenIE KG built independently from passage text. The aligned KG raises paired hit@5 by $0.160$ on a $200$-question split, with a $10{,}000$-resample bootstrap $95\%$ CI of $[0.100,0.220]$. Of $42$ discordant questions, $37$ favor the aligned KG and $5$ the independent KG, giving an exact two-sided sign-test $p=4.4\times10^{-7}$. On a held-out $200$-question split, the effect is $0.115$, with CI $[0.055,0.180]$; $33$ of $43$ discordant questions favor alignment, giving $p=6.1\times10^{-4}$. Of the $37$ aligned-KG flips on the first split, $29$ are BM25 misses, so the swap creates the beyond-lexical wins attributed to the graph channel.

The hybrid retriever fuses lexical, dense, and KG channels by reciprocal-rank fusion; the KG channel ranks passages linked from query-matched nodes. Each candidate pool is the full split passage set ($1{,}256$ and $1{,}229$ passages), and hit@5 requires both gold passages in the top five, under which BM25 attains $0.365$ and $0.355$. BM25 is identical across conditions, and the retriever configuration is fixed and released.

The aligned KG links $342$ corpus passages, all of them gold passages of the split, whereas the independent KG links $1{,}251$ passages, of which $31\%$ are gold. Broader coverage is thus not the source of the gain; the gold-concentrated index is (\S\ref{sec:results}). Because the aligned KG is the gold's source structure, this is a maximal-alignment point rather than a field-wide estimate. The independent arm is itself partly aligned (Appendix~\ref{app:systemside}), so the swap understates the gap to a fully independent KG.

The analyses below and an ordering control probe the robustness of the 2WikiMultiHop swap effect. The win-pattern decomposition of \S\ref{sec:results} and the gold-title coverage statistic of Appendix~\ref{app:systemside} are computed from the per-question retrieval results and the KGs' node sets. Table~\ref{tab:dose} reports the edge-subsample dose--response, and Figure~\ref{fig:e2edose} adds its answer-level counterpart and that of the partial-mapping curve of Table~\ref{tab:qcurve}. As the aligned KG's edge set is subsampled at fraction $f$, the hybrid retriever's hit@5 and paired advantage $\Delta$ over the fixed BM25 baseline change little. For the hybrid retriever, the effect is therefore carried by the aligned inventory and its retained gold-derived node-to-passage mapping rather than by edge connectivity. The PPR-based retrievers behave differently. Table~\ref{tab:zeroedge} deletes every edge for all three retrievers on both splits. PPR and the hybrid-PPR retriever lose the entire gain, because the deleted edges are the evidence triples that lead from the question's entity to the second gold passage.

Table~\ref{tab:samplesize} reports a sample-size sensitivity check. For the first $N$ questions in the split's sampled order at $N\in\{50,100,150,200\}$, it gives the mean paired difference between the aligned KG and the independent OpenIE KG, with a deterministically seeded, $10{,}000$-resample bootstrap $95\%$ CI. The point estimate remains near $0.16$, and the CI excludes zero at every $N$, so the effect is not an artifact of the $200$-question scale.

Table~\ref{tab:swapreplication} reports the cross-retriever replication of \S\ref{sec:results}. Two further graph retrievers are run under the same protocol on both splits. One is a pure PPR retriever that ranks passages from query-matched seed nodes and shares no ranking code with the hybrid system; the other is the hybrid-PPR retriever, a variant of the hybrid retriever whose KG channel is PPR. Both reproduce the swap effect at larger magnitudes than the hybrid retriever. The discordant counts strongly favor the aligned KG: $75$ to $12$ and $73$ to $11$ for PPR, and $75$ to $4$ and $68$ to $1$ for the hybrid-PPR variant. Exact two-sided sign tests give $p=2.7\times10^{-12}$, $2.2\times10^{-12}$, $5.2\times10^{-18}$, and $2.4\times10^{-19}$.

Table~\ref{tab:transplant} reports the inventory-transplant decomposition behind the claim in \S\ref{sec:results} that the transferred gain depends on gold-derived passage links. The transplant condition copies the aligned KG's entity inventory (node identifiers, names, aliases, and gold node-to-passage mapping) into the independent OpenIE graph while keeping the OpenIE edges fixed. The mapping-emptied condition applies the identical transplant and then empties the gold node-to-passage mapping on every transplanted node, leaving names and aliases unchanged. Both are deterministic transformations of the KG, evaluated under the same paired protocol against the OpenIE condition. The transplant transfers a reliable share of the swap gap for PPR on both splits and for the hybrid retriever on the first split. Once the gold mapping is removed, one positive effect of $+0.020$ remains but is nonsignificant, and all significant positive transfers disappear; the only mapping-emptied CI that excludes zero is negative. The reliable transferred share is thus carried by gold-derived passage links rather than lexical surface forms alone.

For the hybrid retriever, the transplant transfers less of the swap gap than the zero-edge condition in Table~\ref{tab:zeroedge} retains, because the transplanted inventory competes for seed mass with roughly ten thousand nodes in the host OpenIE graph. The zero-edge condition instead keeps the aligned inventory pure. For this retriever the two decompositions agree that the carrier is the inventory together with its gold-derived mapping, whether in situ or transplanted. For PPR and hybrid-PPR, which lose the gain when the edges are deleted (Table~\ref{tab:zeroedge}), the carrier also includes the evidence edges. A further control that reorders the OpenIE graph's nodes and edges without changing the inventory leaves every per-question score of the hybrid retriever unchanged. This rules out an ordering artifact in its adjacency construction.

\paragraph{Question type.} Both main 2WikiMultiHop splits hold inference-type questions (``Who is the paternal grandfather of X?''). Under a protocol fixed before any run, we drew a third split of $200$ compositional questions (``Where was the director of film X born?'') from the development set with a fixed seed, disjoint in questions from the other two. Under that protocol we built its corpus, aligned KG, and OpenIE KG with the same code, model, and settings. The corpus has $1{,}155$ passages, of which $48$ and $49$ also occur in the corpora of the first and held-out splits. BM25 reaches $0.215$ hit@5, against $0.365$ and $0.355$ on the inference splits. The aligned KG links $373$ passages, all gold, and the OpenIE KG links $1{,}139$ at a gold fraction of $0.34$.

The aligned KG raises hit@5 by $+0.100$ for the hybrid retriever ($22$ to $2$ discordant questions, $p=3.6\times10^{-5}$), $+0.615$ for PPR ($132$ to $9$, $p=3.6\times10^{-29}$), and $+0.215$ for hybrid-PPR ($44$ to $1$, $p=2.6\times10^{-12}$). HippoRAG~2 gains $+0.200$ (CI $[0.135,0.270]$, $48$ to $8$, $p=4.7\times10^{-8}$; the other CIs are in Table~\ref{tab:swapreplication}). With the aligned KG every graph retriever beats BM25. With the independent KG our three retrievers score $0.210$--$0.240$ against BM25's $0.215$ (all $p\geq0.56$), while HippoRAG~2 scores $0.725$ and exceeds its own dense-only ranking ($0.415$) by $0.310$ ($0.510$ with the aligned KG).

Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-14B answer $0.110$ and $0.080$ of these questions closed-book and $0.555$ and $0.610$ from the gold passages alone. With Qwen2.5-14B and Llama-3.1-8B the EM swap is $+0.345$ and $+0.350$ for PPR and $+0.080$ and $+0.110$ for hybrid-PPR (all $p<0.004$), and $+0.060$ ($p=0.12$) and $+0.105$ ($p=0.005$) for HippoRAG~2. The hybrid retriever, whose retrieval swap is the smallest, has an EM swap of $+0.035$ with both readers ($p=0.17$). EM rises by $+0.27$ to $+0.54$ on the questions whose hit@5 flips from miss to hit and moves by $-0.014$ to $+0.051$ on the rest. The retrieval swap is therefore not specific to inference questions.

These compositional-split cells are reported beside, not inside, the $30$-cell family of \S\ref{sec:results}, Figure~\ref{fig:headline}, and the headroom model of Figure~\ref{fig:cells}. The BGE-M3 runs and the reference-condition and link-count tables (Tables~\ref{tab:readerref} and~\ref{tab:linkcounts}) cover the first and held-out splits only.

\paragraph{Dense-channel backend.} The main runs above use a deterministic lexical surrogate for the dense channel of the hybrid and hybrid-PPR retrievers. Each passage and question is an L2-normalized TF--IDF vector over lower-cased word tokens and their within-word character 3--5-grams, with IDF from the candidate pool. Passages are ranked by cosine similarity, and the channel has no learned parameters.

To check that the swap effect does not depend on that choice, we ran both hybrid retrievers, BM25, and a dense-only retriever on every benchmark and split with the dense channel backed by BGE-M3 embeddings~\citep{chen2024bgem3}. We used the L2-normalized [CLS] embedding, BGE-M3's dense output. Table~\ref{tab:densebge} reports the result. BM25 and the dense-only retriever are bit-identical across KG conditions. The swap effect persists in every 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and GraphRAG-Bench cell, at $0.075$--$0.270$ for the two hybrid retrievers (all $p<2\times10^{-4}$). On STaRK-Prime only the two held-out cells are significant. Dense-only retrieval reaches $0.165$--$0.535$ hit@5 and beats BM25 on 2WikiMultiHop ($0.470$ and $0.450$ against $0.365$ and $0.355$) but not on MetaQA ($0.290$ and $0.265$ against $0.870$ and $0.855$). On 2WikiMultiHop the hybrid retrievers exceed their own dense channel by $0.10$--$0.27$ with the aligned KG and by at most $0.02$ with the independent KG. With a learned dense encoder, the graph channel's gain over dense retrieval thus comes from alignment. The provenance-alignment effect is not an artifact of the surrogate channel.

As a pooling check, mean-pooled BGE-M3 embeddings give a much weaker dense channel (dense-only hit@5 $0.035$--$0.460$) and leave the swap significant in every 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and GraphRAG-Bench cell ($0.080$--$0.295$, $p\leq0.007$). Their outputs are released with the [CLS] ones.

The end-to-end grid was also run on the [CLS] retrieval outputs for both readers. On 2WikiMultiHop the EM swap is positive in all eight reader--split--retriever cells: $+0.025$ to $+0.170$ for hybrid-PPR (three cells significant) and $+0.025$ to $+0.120$ for the hybrid retriever (both Qwen2.5-14B cells significant). On MetaQA the retrieval swap is $+0.075$ to $+0.225$ but the EM swap is $-0.065$ to $+0.020$, with one cell significantly negative, so the retrieval gain again does not reach the answer. On STaRK-Prime no cell is significant. On GraphRAG-Bench the judge-accuracy swap is $+0.005$ to $+0.090$, significant in two of eight cells.

\subsection{Gold-Concentration and Realistic-Structure Controls}
\label{app:concentration}
Two controls test whether the swap effect is carried by the gold concentration of the retrieval structure's node-to-passage index, and whether it survives when the aligned structure is not induced from the evaluated questions. All runs use the main protocol (deterministic dense surrogate, top five, $10{,}000$-resample bootstrap, exact sign test) and are paired against the OpenIE condition of the main runs.

Table~\ref{tab:linkcounts} gives, for every benchmark and split, how many passages each KG links and what fraction of them is gold.

\caption{Passages linked by the aligned and independent KGs, and the gold fraction of the linked passages. Only on STaRK-Prime does the aligned KB link every passage, at the same gold fraction as the OpenIE KG.}

\paragraph{Dilution and concentration on 2WikiMultiHop.} The aligned KG is diluted by attaching links to randomly chosen non-gold passages, at random aligned-KG nodes, until the gold fraction of its linked passage set falls from $1.00$ to $0.75$, $0.50$, and $0.31$, the OpenIE KG's level (seed $20260920$). The OpenIE KG is concentrated by restricting every node's passage links to the split's gold passages while keeping its nodes and edges. Table~\ref{tab:concentration} shows a retriever-dependent picture. For PPR, which diffuses mass over the whole graph, dilution removes the inflation monotonically ($+0.300$, $+0.245$, $-0.030$ on the first split; $+0.300$, $+0.230$, $-0.020$ held-out), and concentrating the independent KG creates it ($+0.215$ and $+0.235$).

For the hybrid and hybrid-PPR retrievers, which start from the nodes a question names, dilution leaves the inflation almost unchanged ($+0.155\to+0.130$; $+0.355\to+0.330$ on the first split). This is because dilution adds non-gold links but removes none, so the gold passages linked from the question's nodes stay in the candidate set. For these retrievers, concentration again creates the inflation ($+0.110$ to $+0.300$). The carrier is thus gold-precise mapping at the question's own entities, which the aligned inventory has by construction; global coverage matters only for the diffusion-based PPR.

\paragraph{Realistic structure on MetaQA.} The MetaQA aligned arm is the KB subgraph induced by the evaluated questions' paths. A practitioner would load the full KB. We build the full-KB arm from every KB triple whose movie is a corpus passage ($9{,}978$ and $10{,}219$ triples, $5{,}414$ and $5{,}491$ nodes, $19{,}910$ and $20{,}390$ edges on the first and held-out splits). Movie nodes map to their own passage and entity nodes to the corpus movies they connect to, so the index covers all $1{,}250$ passages (gold fraction $0.31$). Table~\ref{tab:fullkb} shows that the full KB inflates only PPR ($+0.060$ and $+0.070$, reaching hit@5 $0.995$--$1.000$), leaves the hybrid-PPR retriever at the independent level ($-0.020$, $-0.015$), and lowers the hybrid retriever below both the independent KG and BM25 ($-0.080$, $-0.070$). The aligned arm therefore overstates the inflation for the two hybrid retrievers on MetaQA. The pure graph retriever, whose gold documents are the KB neighbors of the question entity by construction, is inflated by the realistic KB as well.

\paragraph{Realistic structure on 2WikiMultiHop.} Through Wikidata's entity-search and entity-fetch API, we resolve each corpus passage title to a Wikidata item ($1{,}086$ of $1{,}256$ and $1{,}086$ of $1{,}229$ titles resolve, $936$ and $947$ with an exact label match). We fetch every entity-valued statement of those items and build the live-Wikidata arm with $22{,}315$ and $23{,}708$ triples, $11{,}303$ and $11{,}973$ nodes, and $44{,}430$ and $47{,}144$ edges per split. A corpus entity maps to its own passage and an object entity to the corpus passages whose subjects link to it, so the index covers $1{,}086$ passages at gold fraction $0.31$--$0.32$. This is the structure from which the benchmark's evidence triples were drawn, but it is not induced around the evaluated questions.

Table~\ref{tab:wikidata} shows that the live-Wikidata arm inflates only PPR ($+0.075$, $p=0.14$, and $+0.105$, $p=0.038$), whose diffusion from the question entity reaches the bridge entity's page through a Wikidata edge. It leaves the hybrid and hybrid-PPR retrievers at the independent level ($-0.045$ to $+0.020$).

Together with the MetaQA control, this bounds the claim: on 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA, deriving gold from a structure inflates a retriever over that structure to the extent that the structure's node-to-passage index is precise at the question's entities. The question-induced evidence structures that benchmarks release satisfy this condition maximally, a realistic full structure only partly and only for pure PPR.

At the answer level the realistic structures move no cell of our three retrievers significantly by the sign test. With live Wikidata, PPR's EM changes by $+0.060$ and $+0.080$ for Qwen ($p=0.11$, $0.056$; the held-out bootstrap CI, $[0.005,0.155]$, is the only one that excludes zero) and $+0.035$ and $+0.025$ for Llama, and the hybrid retrievers stay within $\pm0.05$. With the full MetaQA KB every cell has $p\geq0.11$.

\subsection{System-Side Alignment}
\label{app:systemside}
Three measurements locate the independent OpenIE condition on the alignment axis between zero and the aligned KG, using only the KGs' node sets and the per-question retrieval results. First, we measure inherited overlap. A node's surface forms are its identifier (without its namespace prefix), name, and aliases, each lower-cased with runs of whitespace collapsed to one space. Two forms match only when the strings are identical, and the gold-title coverage statistic used below applies the same rule. Under this rule, the OpenIE KG contains a surface form of $73.0\%$ and $80.9\%$ of the aligned inventory's $589$ and $593$ nodes on the two splits. Without access to the aligned KG, it also carries a gold node-to-passage link for $33.5\%$ and $34.4\%$ of aligned-KG nodes. At the question level, gold passage titles have mean surface coverage of $0.560$ and $0.613$ and mean mapping coverage of $0.370$ and $0.388$. A KG extracted from passages written from the gold's source structure therefore inherits much of that structure's inventory.

Second, Table~\ref{tab:stratification} reports coverage stratification. Under the OpenIE condition, the per-question hit@5 advantage of every graph retriever over BM25 is monotone in gold-title coverage in all six retriever--split cells: positive when the inventory covers every gold title, smaller under partial coverage, and negative when it covers none. Of the retriever--question events in which a graph retriever hits where BM25 misses, pooled across the three retrievers, $63$ of $72$ on the first split and $66$ of $71$ on the held-out split fall in the covered strata. These strata hold $156$ and $166$ of the $200$ questions. For PPR, the fully-covered-versus-rest differences are $+0.243$, with bootstrap $95\%$ CI $[0.078,0.407]$, and $+0.357$, with CI $[0.181,0.530]$. For the hybrid retriever, the difference is $+0.096$, with CI $[0.014,0.186]$, on the first split; the remaining three cells are directionally consistent, with CIs crossing zero.

This ordering is partly confounded by the BM25 difficulty proxy. BM25 hit@5 is not flat across the three strata but rises as coverage falls, at $0.294$, $0.318$, and $0.568$ on the first split and $0.304$, $0.345$, and $0.500$ on the held-out split. The negative delta in the zero-coverage stratum thus partly reflects reduced headroom over a stronger lexical baseline. The flip counts above nevertheless tie the realized gains to coverage, because the covered strata hold a larger share of the flips than of the questions. Coverage therefore orders the graph advantage, but on this stratification, coverage and lexical difficulty are entangled rather than independent.

Third, Table~\ref{tab:qcurve} reports the partial-mapping dose curve. We re-ran the transplant of Table~\ref{tab:transplant} while restoring the gold node-to-passage mapping on a deterministic fraction $q$ of transplanted nodes. The transferred share moves monotonically from the mapping-emptied floor at $q{=}0$ to the full transplant at $q{=}1$. Together, these measurements show that the inflation mechanism is not restricted to the aligned KG, which is built from the gold's own source structure. A KG that a system builds itself takes part in the mechanism to the extent that its node set overlaps the gold's. A value of $\C_{\kg}=1$ marks maximal derivation on the benchmark side; how much of it a given system realizes is an empirical coverage question.

\subsection{Index Precision and Swap Magnitude}
\label{app:indexprec}
To test what sets the size of the swap, we took, for each question and KG condition, the passages linked from the nodes that the retriever's own matcher returns, and scored them against the gold passages. The predictor, declared before any index value was computed (the declaration is released), is the change in the F1 of these linked passages relative to the OpenIE arm. The outcome is the hit@5 change over all $98$ benchmark--split--retriever--condition cells; hit@5 changes recomputed from the per-question results match the reported ones in every cell. Pooled over benchmarks the association is positive but not established: Spearman $0.51$, with a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.27,0.82]$ from a bootstrap over the four benchmarks, and $0.07$ without 2WikiMultiHop.

Within 2WikiMultiHop, where thirteen KG variants (twelve for the PPR-based retrievers) share questions and gold, the swap tracks the index: $\rho=0.82$ for the hybrid retriever, $0.71$ for hybrid-PPR, and $0.45$ for PPR (question-bootstrap CIs $[0.63,0.87]$, $[0.55,0.80]$, $[0.41,0.52]$). Reliance on the graph scales the effect: hybrid-PPR shares the hybrid retriever's matcher and index yet gains about two to five times as much on 2WikiMultiHop, MetaQA, and GraphRAG-Bench. The direct index is necessary but not sufficient: deleting every edge leaves it unchanged yet collapses PPR, whose second gold passage is reached through an edge.

The question-level index does not order the benchmarks. MetaQA's aligned KG has the largest F1 change at the question's nodes ($+0.61$, hybrid matcher) and GraphRAG-Bench a small one ($+0.09$; precision $0.06$--$0.13$), because GraphRAG-Bench's aligned index is gold-precise only across questions. The question-agnostic gold fraction of the linked passages tracks the pooled swap better (Spearman $0.55$, cluster CI $[0.44,0.77]$).

Two contrasts are explained otherwise. On STaRK-Prime the aligned KB links each entity to its own passage, while questions name entities other than the answer. At the question's nodes the index thus points away from the gold (F1 $0.07$, against $0.02$ with the OpenIE KG), and the swap is null. GraphRAG-Bench swaps more than MetaQA in raw hit@5 because MetaQA's independent arm already reaches $0.860$--$0.935$. As a share of the hit@5 the independent arm leaves, MetaQA's swap exceeds GraphRAG-Bench's for all three retrievers (hybrid, hybrid-PPR, PPR: $0.36$, $0.62$, $1.00$ against $0.13$, $0.48$, $0.56$). With four benchmarks, neither index precision nor $\Chat_{\kg}$ is shown to predict magnitude across benchmarks.

\subsection{Training-Exposure Measurements}
\label{app:memorization}
We measure what each reader knows without retrieval in two ways. Closed-book answering gives the reader the question alone (Table~\ref{tab:readerref}). Evidence-triple recall takes the evidence triples of the $400$ questions of the two 2WikiMultiHop splits ($800$ triple instances, of which $391$ and $392$ are distinct within the two splits and $761$ across both). It asks each of their $760$ distinct subject--relation prompts in the closed-book form ``What is the $\langle$relation$\rangle$ of $\langle$subject$\rangle$?'' at temperature zero. A triple counts as recalled when the normalized prediction equals or contains the object or one of its Wikidata aliases, or, if the prediction is at least four characters long, is contained in one. The readers recall $0.161$ (Llama-3.1-8B) and $0.154$ (Qwen2.5-14B) of the triple instances; most evidence triples are family relations (father, mother, spouse) of historical persons.

Table~\ref{tab:memorization} stratifies the paired EM swap, pooled over the two splits, by closed-book correctness and by evidence recall, and, for GraphRAG-Bench, whose evidence is not a triple set, by closed-book judge correctness. The swap is unchanged on questions the reader cannot answer closed-book and, among these, on questions with an unrecalled triple and on questions with no recalled triple. The complementary strata in which the reader knows the answer or every triple hold only $14$--$22$ questions and are too small for inference. The probe bounds the readers' memory of the gold facts; it does not test memory of the passages or of the benchmark's question--answer pairs, which is in any case constant across arms.

\paragraph{Extractor exposure.} The independent KG is extracted by Llama-3.1-8B, which may have memorized Wikipedia facts, so we test whether it injects gold evidence it could not have read. On 2WikiMultiHop (both splits) the OpenIE KG reproduces $211$ of the $783$ per-split gold evidence triples ($391+392$) with exact endpoints, through $233$ edges. None of these edges has an object missing from the passage it was extracted from (Wilson $95\%$ upper bound $1.6\%$). Among non-gold edges whose object occurs elsewhere in the corpus, $0.4\%$ ($49$ of $11{,}367$) have an object absent from their own source passage ($2.8\%$ of all non-gold edges). On MetaQA none of the $976$ matching edges has an unsupported object. \subsection{Replication on MetaQA}
\label{app:metaqa}
We replicate the controlled swap of \S\ref{sec:results} on a second benchmark to show that the mechanism is not specific to 2WikiMultiHop. MetaQA generates every question from a movie KB, so its retrieval gold has $\C_{\kg}=1.0$ by construction. The KB therefore supplies the aligned arm, which plays the same role as the aligned KG on 2WikiMultiHop. We draw $200$ two-hop questions per split from the released MetaQA test set. We retain questions with exactly two bridge movies whose WikiMovies documents~\citep{miller2016kvmemnn} both exist and at least one of which contains the answer string ($127$ and $124$ of the $200$ questions per split have the answer string in both). Sampling is stratified by relation-pair template under a fixed seed. The two bridge documents are gold, and hit@5 counts a question only when both rank in the top five.

The aligned arm is the KB subgraph induced by the questions' own two-hop paths. The independent arm is an OpenIE KG built over the $1{,}250$-document corpus by the same Llama-3.1-8B pipeline used on 2WikiMultiHop, with no KB or gold access. The lexical baseline is bit-identical across conditions, as verified on every question. Table~\ref{tab:metaqa} reports the result: substituting the aligned KB for the independent KG raises paired hit@5 by $0.035$--$0.085$. The effect is positive in all six retriever--split cells and significant by an exact two-sided sign test in five, with the same retriever ordering as on 2WikiMultiHop.

The effect is smaller than on 2WikiMultiHop mainly because BM25 and the OpenIE arm already reach $0.855$--$0.935$ hit@5 (Appendix~\ref{app:indexprec}). The OpenIE KG also covers $0.958$ and $0.973$ of the gold titles, compared with $0.560$ and $0.613$ on 2WikiMultiHop, so the swap traverses a smaller alignment gap. This high coverage narrows the inventory explanation and is consistent with an additional role for aligned node-to-passage links, the carrier isolated by the mapping-emptied condition on 2WikiMultiHop. It does not eliminate coverage as an explanation: because hit@5 requires both gold documents, the remaining $2.7$--$4.2\%$ title deficit can still affect more questions. The aligned arm has KB access by construction and is an alignment-inflation condition rather than an independent retrieval source; the swap measures the effect of replacing it with a corpus-only KG.

\subsection{Replication at a Non-Maximal Point: STaRK-Prime}
\label{app:stark}
The 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA swaps are run on benchmarks whose KG-derived gold is exactly maximal, $\C_{\kg}=1.0$. To test whether the effect appears away from that extreme, we replicate the swap on STaRK-Prime. Its answer-entity axis receives the methodology-level estimate $\Chat_{\kg}=0.75$ because its gold answer entities are selected by relation traversal over a biomedical KB rather than copied from it. We draw $200$ questions per split with gold answer-entity sets of size one to three and build a $1{,}250$-entity corpus. Each entity is represented by a passage that gives its name and type followed by its description where one exists ($507$ and $396$ of the $1{,}250$ passages carry only the name and type). We then run the identical protocol: the aligned arm is the KB subgraph induced on the corpus, the independent arm is a Llama-3.1-8B OpenIE KG over the same passages, and the lexical baseline is bit-identical across conditions.

Table~\ref{tab:stark} reports the result. The swap is positive in five of six retriever--split cells; the five positive effects range from $0.020$ to $0.065$, but only one cell is significant. The gate fixed before examining the results (a positive swap with an exact paired sign-test $p<0.05$ in a retriever--split cell) is met in one of six cells. We read this non-maximal point as attenuation rather than confirmation: the controlled magnitude is governed by system-side alignment and by how weak retrieval is on this benchmark, not by the census score read as a graded dose. Two features of STaRK-Prime may explain the attenuation. First, its aligned KB subgraph links all $1{,}250$ corpus passages. Unlike on the other three benchmarks, the aligned index is thus not concentrated on the gold (its gold fraction, $20\%$ and $18\%$ on the two splits, equals the OpenIE KG's). Second, because hit@5 requires all one to three gold entities and every retriever is weak here (BM25 $0.135$--$0.215$), the graph channel has little room to separate from BM25.

A post hoc BM25-miss stratum analysis partly separates these explanations. Because the lexical baseline is bit-identical across conditions, the subset of questions that BM25 misses is a condition-independent stratum in which the graph channel has room to help. Restricting to this stratum conditions on a fixed baseline property rather than the swap outcome. Table~\ref{tab:starkheadroom} reports both strata. On the BM25-miss stratum, the swap is positive in all six cells and significant in three by the exact sign test, compared with one of six on the full set. On the complementary BM25-hit stratum, where BM25 already retrieves the gold, the swap is negative for the hybrid and PPR retrievers and positive for the hybrid-PPR retriever. No cell there is significant by the sign test, although the bootstrap CI of the hybrid retriever on the first split, $[-0.333,-0.037]$, excludes zero. This negative control does not reproduce the all-positive BM25-miss pattern, so a universal harder-question explanation is not supported; other differences between the strata remain possible.

The hybrid retriever remains nonsignificant by the sign test on both strata, although its held-out BM25-miss bootstrap CI, $[0.006,0.076]$, excludes zero. The discordant counts are small, and the hybrid-PPR cells in the small BM25-hit stratum are noisy. We therefore treat this analysis as secondary and exploratory rather than as a confirmed endpoint. It indicates that the full-set attenuation is partly explained by the questions BM25 already retrieves, on which the graph channel has no room to help, and not by the mechanism disappearing at $\Chat_{\kg}=0.75$. We present STaRK-Prime as a non-maximal point where the swap is detectable on the BM25-miss stratum but unconfirmed on the full set, not as an established third causal point, and we do not rely on any single cell.

\subsection{Replication on a Text-Corpus Benchmark: GraphRAG-Bench}
\label{app:graphragbench}
GraphRAG-Bench is the only audited benchmark besides 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA whose release exposes both a text corpus and, for every question, the structure-derived evidence from which the gold was isolated (its released per-question evidence relations). The controlled swap can thus be run on a benchmark whose census estimate is non-maximal: $\Chat_{\kg}=0.75$ on the subgraph axis, a bin its construction record supports under the step procedure (Appendix~\ref{app:grbtrace}).

We split the medical corpus into $1{,}584$ consecutive chunks of whole sentences, closing a chunk before it would exceed $120$ words (a single longer sentence forms its own chunk, so $7$ chunks are longer, up to $173$ words). We map each question's released evidence sentences, which paraphrase the corpus, to the chunk with the highest content-token recall. A question is eligible when every evidence sentence maps with recall at least $0.6$ and the union of mapped chunks has size one or two. This holds for $1{,}065$ of the $1{,}896$ Fact Retrieval, Complex Reasoning, and Contextual Summarize questions. Two disjoint $200$-question splits with identical type composition ($90$/$85$/$25$) are drawn with a fixed seed, and hit@5 counts a question only when every gold chunk ranks in the top five. The full $1{,}584$-chunk corpus is the candidate pool for both splits.

The aligned arm is a KG induced from the benchmark's own structure. The evidence relations of the split's questions are grouped by gold chunk and passed through the same OpenIE extractor used for the independent arm (Llama-3.1-8B, temperature zero), so every node's passage mapping is gold-derived. Because the benchmark does not release its ontology graph, this arm approximates the mined subgraph rather than reproducing it. The independent arm is the OpenIE KG built once over all $1{,}584$ chunks with no access to questions or evidence. The lexical baseline is bit-identical across conditions, as verified on every question.

Table~\ref{tab:graphragbench} reports the result: the swap is positive in all six retriever--split cells and significant in all six by the exact sign test. The swap is smallest for the hybrid retriever, larger for hybrid-PPR, and largest for PPR, whereas on 2WikiMultiHop and MetaQA the hybrid-PPR swap is the largest. Under the independent KG the PPR and hybrid-PPR retrievers score below BM25 and the hybrid retriever is within $0.02$ of it, so on this benchmark the graph-over-lexical conclusion depends on provenance alignment. The aligned KG links all $202$ and $208$ gold chunks of its split and no other chunk, whereas the independent KG links $1{,}581$ of the $1{,}584$ chunks. The gain therefore comes from gold-derived node-to-passage mapping rather than broader passage coverage, the carrier isolated by the mapping-emptied condition on 2WikiMultiHop.

\paragraph{Judge validation.} Because GraphRAG-Bench gold answers are sentences, the end-to-end answers on this benchmark are graded by an LM judge (Mistral-Small-24B, temperature zero) that sees the question, the reference answer, and the candidate. A rater who is not an author graded $100$ sampled judge decisions, $50$ marked correct and $50$ incorrect by the judge, from the same three texts and blind to the verdict. Human and judge agree on $86$ of $100$ ($\kappa=0.72$). In $13$ of the $14$ disagreements, the human accepted a candidate as stating the reference's key fact in fewer words and the judge rejected it, so the judge's precision against the human is $0.98$ and its recall $0.79$. Against this rater the judge is strict, and the same criterion is applied to both arms of every swap. Because the sample is stratified $50$/$50$ by the judge's verdict, these precision and recall values describe the sample rather than the full set of verdicts.

As an LM check, three Claude models (Claude Opus~5, Claude Sonnet~5, and Claude Haiku~4.5) labeled the same $100$ items, one request per item and no other context. Their system prompt was a rater guide that extends the human rater's rule with explicit INCORRECT cases (contradicting additions, uncommitted alternatives, missing mechanisms). They agree with the judge on $87$, $88$, and $81$ of $100$ ($\kappa=0.74$, $0.76$, $0.62$), their majority on $87$ ($\kappa=0.74$), and with the human rater on $81$, $82$, and $69$. Fleiss' $\kappa$ among the three is $0.74$. Unlike the first human rater, they are stricter than the judge: against their majority the judge's precision is $0.80$ and its recall $0.93$. The replies, model identifiers, and scoring are released.

Three non-author raters, two of the human raters of Appendix~\ref{app:humanraters} and a graduate student in data science and NLP, labeled the same $100$ items with the same guide under a protocol fixed in advance. Each worked alone and without AI assistance, in an independently drawn order, with two attention items, which all three passed. Four raters were asked: these two, a third rater, and, as a backup, the graduate student, who received a separate blank copy of the third rater's workbook. The third rater's label set was identical to the second rater's on all $100$ items and was excluded under the protocol. After a first scoring run, the graduate student's set, completed separately, was used in its place. They agree with the judge on $90$ of $100$, $87$ of $99$, and $82$ of $99$ labeled items ($\kappa=0.80$, $0.76$, and $0.66$), their majority on $88$ of $100$ ($\kappa=0.76$), and with one another at Fleiss' $\kappa=0.79$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.69,0.89]$). Like the Claude models, they are stricter than the judge: against their majority its precision is $0.80$ and its recall $0.95$. The judge therefore sits between the lenient reading of the first rater and the stricter readings of the human and LM raters under the explicit guide.

As a second judge we graded each reader's answers with the other reader, so that no answer is graded by the model that produced it. Qwen2.5-14B (judging Llama-3.1-8B's answers) and Llama-3.1-8B (judging Qwen2.5-14B's) agree with the primary judge on $0.85$ ($\kappa=0.70$) and $0.79$ ($\kappa=0.55$) of $2{,}489$ and $2{,}520$ unique question--answer pairs. Qwen is stricter and Llama more lenient than Mistral-Small. The swap pattern is stable across judges. The PPR first-split cell is positive and significant under every judge for both readers ($+0.120$ to $+0.175$ for Llama, $+0.155$ to $+0.220$ for Qwen). The PPR held-out cell is positive under every judge ($+0.035$ to $+0.100$) but significant only for Qwen's answers under the primary judge. The hybrid-PPR cells are positive under every judge and significant under two of the three judges, and the hybrid cells stay between $-0.025$ and $+0.050$ with no significant cell. The human labels, judge verdicts, and cross-judge outputs are released.

\section{Released Resources and Prompts}
\subsection{Provenance-Audited Reference Benchmark}
\label{app:protocol}
We release provenance-audited benchmark records that serve as a $\C_a=0$ reference, the lower calibration anchor of the census, and a construction template. In this template, an LM generates questions from source passages, a cross-family verifier checks them (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), a lexical-overlap filter gates leakage, gold passages are provenance-tagged to source text, and the construction code has no access to any retriever or KG index. The benchmark spans four manufacturing domains in English. The single-hop set has $112$ items with $111$ distinct question strings; its questions were generated by Mistral-Small-24B, run locally, and checked by Gemini 2.5 Flash as the primary verifier. A blind $40$-item cross-family spot-check of this verification protocol, with Claude Haiku~4.5 (a model, not a human) as the second rater, gives $35$ non-abstained comparisons, $29/35$ exact agreement, and $\kappa=0.79$. It checks the protocol rather than duplicating the verification of every released single-hop item.

The $13$-question two-hop slice is built from text-only bridge pairs and shows that multi-hop gold can be constructed without reading a KG. Its questions were generated by a Claude Sonnet model and checked by two verifiers, Gemini 2.5 Flash and Qwen2.5-14B, and kept only when both accepted them ($\kappa=0.64$ between the two over the verification set). The labels, prompts, and agreement calculation are released.

On the $112$ single-hop items, BM25 reaches hit@5 $0.938$, the lexical dense surrogate of the main runs (Appendix~\ref{app:swaprobust}) $0.688$, and the hybrid retriever over a KG built from the same manufacturing corpus $0.741$. Lexical retrieval is thus near ceiling, and these values are reference points only. The two-hop slice is a small demonstration rather than a powered retrieval evaluation and is imbalanced across three represented domains ($10$ textile, $2$ steel, $1$ construction, and no semiconductor item). The upper calibration anchor is a template-built question set over that KG. The anchor's gold entities, edge, and path are the endpoint identifiers and triple of a KG edge, copied from the KG by code, and its answer text fills a template with the edge's relation phrase. The anchor serves only to pin the top of the census scale. Source passage text is not redistributed. Identifiers and URLs are released, but reproducing retrieval requires obtaining the source passages from their publishers.

\subsection{Scoring and Generation Prompts}
\label{app:prompts}
The prompts below are reproduced verbatim from the released code, except that the two-hop generator prompt marks where it repeats the domain constraint and few-shot examples of the single-hop prompt. The primary census raters applied the full rubric of Appendix~\ref{app:rubricfull}. The first box is the short rater prompt from which the fourth, short-prompt human rater (Appendix~\ref{app:humanraters}) and the cross-model LMs (Appendix~\ref{app:crossmodel}) worked. The remaining boxes are the generation and verification prompts of the released benchmark.

\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, colback=gray!3, colframe=gray!55, colbacktitle=black!75, coltitle=white, boxrule=0.6pt, title=\textbf{Short provenance-circularity rater prompt (cross-model LMs and short-prompt human rater)}, fonttitle=\small\bfseries]
\footnotesize
You are scoring how a question-answering benchmark's retrieval GOLD was constructed, on a five-level provenance-circularity scale. K = the evaluation knowledge graph or base that a graph retriever ranks over. A = the answer surface, the spans where the answer string appears.\\
bin 0: human or source-text gold, independent of K and A.\\
bin 1: human or text gold with KG-guided or answer-guided selection.\\
bin 2: auto-generated and then human-validated.\\
bin 3: auto-generated from KG triples or templates, OR evidence retained by answer-string distant supervision.\\
bin 4: gold copied directly from the evaluation KG, OR selected solely because it contains the answer string.\\[4pt]
Benchmark: <name>\\
How its gold was constructed: <description>\\[4pt]
Reply with ONLY one integer from 0 to 4 (the bin). No other text.
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent The prompt states bin~2 without the rubric's $\kg$-or-$A$ qualifier of Step~2 (Appendix~\ref{app:rubricfull}); no census bin is bin~2.

\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, colback=gray!3, colframe=gray!55, colbacktitle=black!75, coltitle=white, boxrule=0.6pt, title=\textbf{Released-benchmark single-hop generator system prompt}, fonttitle=\small\bfseries]
\footnotesize
You are a careful benchmark author for a manufacturing-domain retrieval benchmark. You read a research paper passage and write a single QA item whose answer is derivable ONLY from the given passage text. You are given NO knowledge graph and must not invent facts.\\[4pt]
The passages come from manufacturing-domain research papers (semiconductor, construction, steel, or textile). Your question MUST be about manufacturing-technical content: a process step, material property, equipment specification, defect or failure mode, process parameter, or measured quantity. FORBIDDEN - do NOT write questions about: (a) paper metadata (author names, affiliations, corresponding author, venue, publication date, funding, acknowledgements); (b) generic background knowledge unrelated to the domain (e.g. AlexNet, ImageNet, generic ML architectures, mathematical definitions); (c) paper structure (section numbers, related work summaries). If the passage contains ONLY metadata or generic background with no domain-specific technical fact, output the single word SKIP and nothing else - do NOT produce a JSON object.\\[4pt]
Examples of FORBIDDEN questions (output SKIP for these passages):\\
\hspace*{1em}BAD: `Which institution is the corresponding author affiliated with?' -> answer would be an affiliation string - metadata, SKIP.\\
\hspace*{1em}BAD: `What neural network architecture was first introduced for large-size datasets?' -> answer is AlexNet - generic ML background, SKIP.\\
\hspace*{1em}BAD: `What does Section 3 describe?' -> paper structure, SKIP.\\
Examples of GOOD questions (manufacturing-technical):\\
\hspace*{1em}GOOD (factual): `What property of the coating layer is measured in this study?' -> answer: `corrosion resistance' from passage.\\
\hspace*{1em}GOOD (paraphrase): `What parameter governs the thickness uniformity of the deposited film?' -> answer surface form differs from question wording.\\
\hspace*{1em}GOOD (comparison): `How does the annealing temperature in Process A compare with that in Process B as described in the passage?'\\[4pt]
Output rules when writing a question: (1) the answer must be a short span quoted verbatim from the passage; (2) the answer surface form must NOT appear verbatim in the question - paraphrase or use an alias/synonym instead; (3) reply with a single JSON object and nothing else, using exactly the keys: question, answer, evidence. `evidence' is the exact passage substring that supports the answer.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, colback=gray!3, colframe=gray!55, colbacktitle=black!75, coltitle=white, boxrule=0.6pt, title=\textbf{Released-benchmark two-hop bridge generator system prompt}, fonttitle=\small\bfseries]
\footnotesize

Output rules when writing a question: (1) the answer must be a short span quoted from one of the passages; (2) the bridge entity must NOT appear verbatim in the question; (3) reply with a single JSON object and nothing else, using exactly the keys: question, answer, bridge, evidence\_a, evidence\_b. `evidence\_a'/`evidence\_b' are exact substrings from passage A and B.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, colback=gray!3, colframe=gray!55, colbacktitle=black!75, coltitle=white, boxrule=0.6pt, title=\textbf{Released-benchmark single-hop verifier system prompt}, fonttitle=\small\bfseries]
\footnotesize
You are a careful annotator linking a surface mention in a research excerpt to a knowledge-graph node. Read the excerpt and the mention, then choose the single best matching node from the numbered candidates. If none fit, choose NONE. Reply with the index of the best candidate or NONE. Use exactly the format INDEX: <n> on the final line. Do not output any other commentary.

The prompt above is the one the verifier received.
